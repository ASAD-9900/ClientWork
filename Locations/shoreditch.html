Architectural Guidance · Shoreditch

How Can You Make a Shoreditch Warehouse Conversion Feel More Comfortable?

Create smaller, purposeful settings within the open space.

A generous floor area can still feel unsettled when furniture is positioned without a clear relationship between activities.

We explore seating groups, dining positions and storage alongside the existing architecture. Rugs, upholstery and curtains can introduce softness, while lighting helps distinguish areas for relaxing, eating and working.

The aim is to retain the openness you enjoy while making the room feel comfortable at an everyday scale.

Layout & Spatial Optimisation · Shoreditch

How Can You Add Storage Without Hiding Exposed Brickwork or Original Features?

Identify the features you want to emphasise, then plan storage around them.

A freestanding cabinet, low media unit or fitted cupboard within a recess may provide useful storage while leaving the main architectural elements visible.

We consider the items you need to accommodate before choosing open shelving, closed cupboards or a combination of both.

Dimensions, access and the condition of existing surfaces help determine the most appropriate solution. Any fixings or alterations should be assessed for the individual property.

Joinery, Materials & Details · Shoreditch

How Can a Shoreditch Bar or Café Feel Distinctive While Remaining Practical?

Build the identity around a clear concept and a workable layout.

Colour, furniture and lighting should support the kind of experience you want customers to have. A relaxed daytime café needs different arrangements from an evening lounge or a venue that serves both purposes.

We consider seating options, counter positions and service routes before developing the material palette.

Finishes should suit the expected wear and cleaning requirements. Where neighbouring properties or specialist equipment affect the brief, the appropriate technical input should inform the design.
