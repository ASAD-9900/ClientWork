Architectural Guidance · London Bridge

How Can a London Bridge Office Balance Collaboration and Quiet Work?

Plan around the activities your team carries out.

Open desks may suit some tasks, while calls, confidential discussions and concentrated work need different conditions.

We discuss headcount, attendance patterns, meeting frequency and equipment before exploring the layout. A combination of shared work areas, enclosed rooms and quieter settings may provide a more useful arrangement than repeating the same workstation throughout.

Furniture, lighting and acoustic requirements should develop alongside the space plan.

Layout & Spatial Optimisation · London Bridge

Can You Improve Meeting Privacy Without Making an Office Feel Enclosed?

Yes, but the whole room needs consideration.

Glazing can preserve visual openness, while suitable partitions, doors and finishes contribute to privacy. Sound can also travel through ceilings, ventilation routes and gaps around adjoining elements.

We establish the intended use of each meeting space and coordinate relevant specialist input where required. Product ratings alone should not be treated as a guarantee of privacy in the completed room.

Joinery, Materials & Details · London Bridge

How Can You Retain Character in a Converted Warehouse Interior?

Let the existing features inform the design.

Brickwork, columns, beams and generous ceiling heights can influence furniture scale, lighting and material choices.

We consider which features to highlight and how new storage, partitions and furniture can sit comfortably alongside them. The condition of existing fabric and any proposed alterations should be assessed by the appropriate specialists.

A restrained palette can help the original structure remain a strong part of the interior.
