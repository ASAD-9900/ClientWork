Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions. Detailed architectural coordination was resolved prior to site commencement using precise kitchen renovation and spatial layout.

01 Challenge

Connecting Rooms with Different Functions

A detached house can lose its sense of continuity when individual rooms are designed separately.

Design Response

Establish a shared palette of timber tones, complementary colours and selected finishes. Vary the furniture, lighting and textures to give each room its own character within that framework.

02 Challenge

Balancing Formal Reception Rooms with Everyday Comfort

A reception room needs to feel welcoming as well as visually composed. Furniture that is poorly proportioned can restrict movement or leave seating arrangements disconnected.

Design Response

Develop furniture layouts around conversation, clear circulation and the room’s focal points. Check furniture dimensions before ordering and combine decorative lighting with practical reading and ambient light.

03 Challenge

Making the Kitchen Practical and Visually Connected

A kitchen must accommodate preparation, cooking, storage and cleaning while relating to the surrounding interiors.

Design Response

Resolve the layout and appliance requirements before finalising cabinetry. Use the walnut finish as a visual anchor, supported by worktops, handles and lighting selected for everyday use and maintenance.

04 Challenge

Creating a Restful Principal Bedroom Suite

A bedroom can feel crowded when storage and furniture are considered too late.

Design Response

Establish the bed position, wardrobe requirements and circulation first. Develop lighting and finishes around those decisions to create a comfortable, uncluttered setting.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Project Cost?

For initial planning, a comparable substantial refurbishment could be assessed against a provisional allowance of £200,000–£450,000 excluding VAT.

This is an illustrative starting range, rather than this property’s recorded expenditure or a quotation. It assumes work across the principal rooms described, including kitchen replacement, selected fitted joinery, finishes, lighting, furniture and installation.

  • Interior design and technical information: Rooms included, drawing requirements and level of coordination
  • Refurbishment works: Preparation, decoration, flooring and extent of services alterations
  • Kitchen: Cabinetry, worktops, appliances and installation
  • Fitted joinery: Quantity, dimensions, internal fittings and finishes
  • Furniture and lighting: Items retained, new purchases and specification
  • Procurement and installation: Delivery, assembly, site coordination and final placement

The main cost drivers are the size of the house, extent of refurbishment, kitchen specification, quantity of joinery and furniture selections.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Should a Similar Project Take?

7–12 months

For a substantial refurbishment of the rooms described, an initial programme might allow approximately 7–12 months, with several stages overlapping.

  • Brief, measured survey and initial budget: 2–4 weeks
  • Space planning and concept design: 4–8 weeks
  • Detailed design, specification and tendering: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals, where required: Dependent on the proposed work
  • Procurement and joinery manufacture: 8–16 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Refurbishment and installation: 12–20 weeks
  • Final styling, snagging and handover: 1–3 weeks
