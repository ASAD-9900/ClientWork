A More Organised Approach to Family Living

This Wimbledon residence scheme connects shared living spaces with quieter private rooms through coordinated joinery, warm neutral colours and considered lighting.

The design addresses everyday family needs: somewhere to put household belongings, comfortable spaces for relaxing and entertaining, and a bedroom suite with clearly organised clothing storage.

In the living room, a media wall combines display shelving with closed cabinetry. The principal suite brings together fitted wardrobes, a dressing area and a coordinated bathroom, while the entrance establishes a calm, welcoming arrival.

Commissioned within Kingston upon Thames and serving clients across nearby Richmond upon Thames, this project reflects our studio's architectural approach to residential interior decor and styling.

The Design Brief

The supplied brief centres on improving circulation, daylight and storage while accommodating both family routines and entertaining.

  • Create a clearer relationship between the ground-floor living spaces.
  • Make household storage part of the interior design.
  • Provide a comfortable reception room with integrated media equipment.
  • Organise the principal suite around sleeping, dressing and bathing.
  • Select finishes appropriate for frequent family use.
  • The design approach considers how the house functions throughout the day, from busy mornings to quieter evenings.
Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions. Detailed architectural coordination was resolved prior to site commencement using precise garden and landscape design.

01 Challenge

Connecting Ground-Floor Spaces

A family home needs clear routes between the entrance, kitchen, dining area and garden-facing rooms.

Design Response

Develop furniture layouts alongside circulation, checking door swings and routes through each space. Assess proposed alterations against the existing structure and services before finalising the design.

02 Challenge

Keeping the Living Room Organised

Media equipment, books, toys and decorative objects place different demands on storage.

Design Response

Combine open shelving for selected items with closed cupboards for everyday belongings. Coordinate equipment dimensions, ventilation, cable routes and maintenance access before cabinetry is manufactured.

03 Challenge

Creating a Practical Dressing Room

A dressing room can look generous while providing unsuitable storage or restricted movement.

Design Response

Establish the client’s hanging, drawer, shoe and accessory requirements first. Check wardrobe openings and circulation before introducing a dressing table, seating or central island.

04 Challenge

Coordinating the Bedroom and Bathroom

The principal suite needs to feel connected without compromising privacy or practical access.

Design Response

Plan the bed, wardrobes, dressing area and bathroom entrance together. Bathroom layouts should resolve usable clearances, storage, ventilation and maintenance requirements before finishes are selected.

05 Challenge

Choosing Finishes for Daily Use

Kitchen, entrance and reception areas experience different levels of wear and moisture exposure.

Design Response

Review flooring and surface samples against their intended use, cleaning requirements and supplier guidance. Where underfloor heating is proposed, confirm compatibility with the selected floor build-up and finish.

These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site discoveries, structural changes and client-requested revisions require confirmation from project records.

Budget & Delivery

Indicative Investment & Programme Guide

Transparent cost planning, realistic schedule benchmarks, and coordinated turnkey execution.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Wimbledon Refurbishment Cost?

£180,000–£400,000 excluding VAT.

This is an initial allowance for a substantial internal refurbishment involving an agreed combination of kitchen and bathroom improvements, fitted joinery, flooring, decoration, lighting, furniture and installation.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, drawings and specifications
  • Internal refurbishment: Rooms included, preparation, flooring and decoration
  • Kitchen: Extent of replacement, cabinetry, worktops and appliances
  • Bathrooms: Number of rooms, fittings, finishes and associated works
  • Fitted joinery: Media cabinetry, wardrobes and dressing furniture
  • Building services: Defined electrical, plumbing and heating alterations
  • Furniture and lighting: Specification, procurement, delivery and installation

Extensions, major structural alterations, external works and whole-house services replacement are excluded from this illustrative scope.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

6–11 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 6–11 months for a substantial internal refurbishment, with some stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and initial budget: 2–4 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 4–8 weeks
  • Detailed design, specification and tendering: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals, where required: Confirm for the proposed work
  • Procurement and joinery manufacture: 8–16 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Refurbishment and installation: 12–20 weeks
  • Final styling, snagging and handover: 2–3 weeks
