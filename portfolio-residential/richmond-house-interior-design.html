A Family Home Connected to Its Garden

This Richmond house scheme centres on a welcoming ground floor with a stronger relationship between the interior and garden.

The design brings together sociable kitchen and dining spaces with a quieter living area. Muted green tones reference the surrounding planting, while walnut finishes, pale stone textures and warm metallic details introduce contrast.

Storage, furniture placement and lighting help define the different settings. The aim is a home that accommodates busy family routines while remaining comfortable for quieter evenings and entertaining.

The Design Brief

The supplied brief focuses on creating a family home with better-connected shared spaces, useful storage and a coordinated interior palette.

  • Strengthen the relationship between the kitchen, dining area and garden.
  • Provide distinct settings for family activities and relaxation.
  • Integrate storage into the architecture of the rooms.
  • Coordinate furniture, lighting and finishes throughout.
  • Balance visual character with practical maintenance.
  • The garden connection informs the layout and colour direction, while the living spaces provide a warmer, more sheltered setting within the house.

Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions. Detailed architectural coordination was resolved prior to site commencement using precise landscape and terrace design.

01 Challenge

Balancing Sociable Spaces with Quieter Living

A large connected ground floor needs to accommodate cooking, conversation and relaxation without every activity competing for the same space.

Design Response

Establish clear zones through furniture placement, joinery and focal features. A fireplace can provide visual separation, but acoustic privacy requires a separate assessment and appropriate physical separation where needed.

02 Challenge

Making Garden Access Work with Furniture Layouts

Large glazed openings can improve the outlook, but furniture must allow comfortable access between the house and terrace.

Design Response

Plan dining positions, seating and circulation together. Check door operation, furniture clearances and routes to the garden before finalising the arrangement.

03 Challenge

Providing Storage for Everyday Family Life

An attractive interior needs somewhere for the belongings that accumulate through daily use.

Design Response

Combine closed cupboards with selected display shelving. Kitchen storage should be planned around food, appliances, preparation equipment and recycling, while living-room cabinetry can accommodate books and media equipment.

04 Challenge

Selecting Practical Stone and Timber Finishes

Natural materials vary in their maintenance needs and suitability for different uses.

Design Response

Confirm the intended application before choosing the material. Worktops, bathroom surfaces and flooring should be assessed for cleaning, moisture exposure, staining and the level of maintenance the household is comfortable undertaking.

05 Challenge

Coordinating Proposed Alterations with the Existing House

Changes to openings, floors and services can affect the design, cost and programme.

Design Response

Establish existing conditions through surveys and technical coordination before work begins. Structural alterations and heavy fixtures require project-specific assessment rather than assumptions based on appearance.

Budget & Delivery

Indicative Investment & Programme Guide

Transparent cost planning, realistic schedule benchmarks, and coordinated turnkey execution.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Richmond Refurbishment Cost?

£250,000–£600,000 excluding VAT.

This broad allowance applies to a substantial internal refurbishment with an agreed combination of kitchen and bathroom replacement, fitted joinery, internal finishes, services alterations, furniture, lighting and installation.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, drawings and specifications
  • Internal refurbishment: Preparation, flooring, decoration and agreed alterations
  • Kitchen: Cabinetry, worktops, appliances and installation
  • Bathrooms: Number of rooms, fittings, finishes and associated works
  • Fitted joinery: Storage, shelving, media cabinetry and internal fittings
  • Building services: Defined electrical, plumbing and heating works
  • Furniture and lighting: Specification, procurement, delivery and installation
  • A rear extension is outside this allowance and requires a separate cost plan covering its construction, structure, glazing, services and finishes. Major structural works, landscaping and external refurbishment are also excluded.: Professional fees, surveys and approval-related charges should be identified separately. Allow a contingency of 10–15%, reviewed following survey, with VAT added where applicable.
02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

8–14 months

For a substantial internal refurbishment, an initial programme could allow approximately 8–14 months, with several stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and feasibility: 3–5 weeks
  • Space planning and concept design: 4–8 weeks
  • Detailed design, specification and tendering: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals, where required: Confirm for the proposed scope
  • Procurement and joinery manufacture: 10–18 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Refurbishment and installation: 16–24 weeks
  • Final styling, snagging and handover: 2–3 weeks
Managing Design Changes

Layouts, kitchen arrangements, joinery drawings and principal finishes should be approved before manufacture or installation.
