Luxury Residential Interior Architecture

Classic Character with a Contemporary Approach to Family Living

This Barnet family residence combines traditional architectural features with a warmer, more contemporary interior palette.

Fireplace details, ceiling mouldings and the timber staircase provide character, while green joinery, upholstered furniture and contrasting textures introduce a relaxed atmosphere. The design connects the formal reception spaces with the more informal kitchen and dining areas, giving each room its own purpose within a coordinated home.

Olive and sage tones sit alongside terracotta accents, timber finishes and soft textiles. In the bathroom, a walnut-toned vanity and brass details continue the same warm design direction.

Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions.

01 Challenge

Preserving Character While Updating the Interior

Traditional features can feel disconnected from newer rooms when finishes and proportions are considered separately.

Design Response

The design approach retains the fireplace and architectural detailing as focal points, pairing them with simpler furniture forms and coordinated colours. This allows the house’s character to remain visible within a more contemporary setting.

02 Challenge

Connecting Formal and Informal Rooms

A family house needs spaces for different activities without the interior feeling fragmented.

Design Response

A shared palette connects the reception rooms, kitchen and dining areas. Furniture arrangements give each room a clear function, while repeated timber tones and green accents create continuity.

03 Challenge

Balancing Display with Everyday Storage

Open shelving can add character, but family living also requires somewhere to put everyday belongings out of sight.

Design Response

The joinery approach combines display shelving with closed cupboards. For a similar project, internal layouts would be developed around the household’s actual storage needs, including books, games, media equipment and frequently used items.

04 Challenge

Improving the Relationship with the Garden

Garden-facing rooms need furniture layouts that preserve access and make the most of the outlook.

Design Response

The design approach considers dining positions, circulation and the transition to the terrace together. External furniture and finishes should complement the interior while being suitable for outdoor use.

05 Challenge

These are design challenges addressed by the scheme’s approach. Specific site conditions, structural interventions and changes during construction require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Barnet Refurbishment Cost?

£150,000–£300,000 excluding VAT.

This guide applies to a substantial internal refurbishment across selected principal rooms, potentially including kitchen replacement, bathroom improvements, fitted joinery, flooring, decoration, lighting, furniture and installation.

  • Design and technical information: Rooms, layouts, drawings and specification requirements
  • Internal refurbishment: Preparation, decoration, flooring and agreed services alterations
  • Kitchen: Cabinetry, worktops, appliances and installation
  • Bathrooms: Sanitaryware, fittings, finishes and installation
  • Fitted joinery: Dimensions, internal storage, materials and finishes
  • Furniture and lighting: Items retained, new purchases and delivery
  • Project coordination: Procurement, installation and agreed site involvement
  • Floor area, property condition, kitchen specification and the quantity of fitted joinery will materially affect the budget.: VAT should be added where applicable. Allow a separate contingency of 10–15%, reviewed once the property has been surveyed and the scope developed. Professional fees, statutory charges and external works should be identified explicitly in the tailored cost plan.
02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

6–10 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 6–10 months for the internal refurbishment described, with some stages overlapping.

  • Brief, survey and initial budget: 2–4 weeks
  • Space planning and concept design: 4–6 weeks
  • Detailed design, specification and tendering: 4–8 weeks
  • Procurement and joinery manufacture: 8–16 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Internal refurbishment and installation: 10–16 weeks
  • Final styling, snagging and handover: 1–2 weeks
  • An extension would require a separate programme allowing for design, relevant approvals, structural coordination and construction.: The final schedule also depends on contractor availability, confirmed product lead times and whether the family remains in the house during the works.
