Hotel Design Starts Before the Guest Reaches the Bedroom
Think About the Complete Guest Journey
A hotel is experienced as a sequence of spaces.
The guest arrives.
They enter the reception.
They check in.
They move through corridors and lifts.
They enter the bedroom.
They use the bathroom.
They may visit the restaurant, bar, lounge, spa or other shared spaces.
Each part contributes to the overall impression of the property.
That is why hotel interior design shouldn't be treated as a collection of unrelated rooms.
We consider how the different spaces relate to one another while allowing each area to perform its own function.

Design Around Your Guest
Who Is the Hotel For?
A boutique city hotel may need a very different interior from an extended-stay property or destination retreat.
Before developing the design, we want to understand:
Who is your typical guest?
Why are they staying?
How long do they usually stay?
Is the hotel positioned around business, leisure or both?
What should guests remember about the property?
What level of service is being provided?
Which spaces generate the most activity?
What is the existing brand direction?
Is the hotel independent or part of a wider brand?
What is changing as part of the project?
These questions help establish the interior direction before furniture and finishes are selected.

Hotel Interior Design Questions
What does a hotel interior designer do?
A hotel interior designer develops the interior around the guest experience, brand, space and operational requirements. This can include guest rooms, reception, public spaces, furniture, lighting, materials, bespoke joinery and other agreed areas.
What areas of a hotel can Oraanj design?
Depending on the project, Oraanj can design guest rooms, suites, reception areas, lobbies, lounges, restaurants, bars and other agreed hospitality spaces.
Can you redesign hotel bedrooms without refurbishing the entire hotel?
Yes. A project can focus on guest rooms or another selected part of the property rather than the complete hotel.
Do you design boutique hotels?
Yes. Boutique and independent hotels can be particularly suited to a bespoke design approach because the interior can be developed around the individual property and guest proposition.
Do you design serviced apartments?
Yes. Oraanj can design serviced accommodation and furnished apartments where residential comfort needs to work alongside hospitality requirements.
Can you design hotel restaurants and bars?
Yes. Restaurant and bar design can form part of a wider hotel project or be discussed as an individual hospitality space.
Can you design bespoke hotel furniture and joinery?
Yes. Bespoke joinery can include wardrobes, headboards, desks, media units, reception desks, bars and other fitted interior elements according to the project.
Can you provide hotel FF&E and procurement?
Yes. FF&E selection and procurement can form part of the agreed hotel interior design service.
Do you provide 3D visualisation for hotels?
Yes. 3D interior visualisation can be used to communicate proposed guest rooms and key public spaces before implementation.
Can Oraanj refurbish the hotel as well as design it?
For suitable projects, Oraanj can discuss continuing from interior design into refurbishment, fit-out and project delivery according to the agreed scope.
Can you work with our existing architect and consultants?
Yes. Oraanj can work as part of a wider professional project team, with responsibilities agreed between the relevant parties.
Can a hotel be refurbished while it remains open?
Potentially, but this depends on the property and the extent of the works. Phasing, access, noise, programme and operational requirements need to be considered with the hotel operator and wider project team.
How much does hotel interior design cost?
Hotel projects are individually scoped because fees depend on factors such as the number of rooms, public areas, design requirements, technical detail, FF&E and whether refurbishment or project delivery is required.
Do you work outside London?
Oraanj is London-based and can discuss suitable hotel and hospitality projects elsewhere in the UK and selected international locations.
