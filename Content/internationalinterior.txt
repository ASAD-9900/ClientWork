Your London Team While You're Abroad
Managing an interior project from another country can quickly become complicated.
There are designers to speak to, materials to approve, suppliers to coordinate, furniture to order, deliveries to organise and decisions that sometimes need to be made while you are thousands of miles away.
Oraanj provides a central London-based team to help coordinate these different stages.
We work with you remotely through video meetings, digital presentations, drawings, 3D visuals, material selections and agreed project updates.
Where the scope extends into renovation and installation, our wider team can also coordinate the relevant delivery stages in London.
The aim is to give you clear visibility of the project without requiring you to travel to London for every decision.

How Does Interior Design Work When You Live Overseas?
01 — Private Video Consultation
We start with a video meeting to understand the property, your requirements, lifestyle, design preferences, budget expectations and intended programme.
If the property is in London, our team can arrange the appropriate site review as part of the agreed service.
02 — Site Information & Space Planning
We review the property information and develop layouts around the way you want to use the space.
You can review proposals remotely without needing to travel to London.
03 — Digital Design Development
Design concepts, materials, furniture, lighting and other key elements are presented digitally.
3D visualisation can also help you understand how the completed interior is intended to look.
04 — Remote Approvals
Feedback and approvals are managed through scheduled online meetings and digital presentations.
Where appropriate, physical material or finish samples can also be discussed as part of the approval process.
05 — Technical Development
Once the design is agreed, the required technical information can be prepared according to the project scope.
06 — Costing & Procurement
The approved design provides a clearer basis for sourcing, supplier quotations and the wider project budget.
Furniture, lighting, finishes and bespoke elements can then be planned around availability and lead times.
07 — Renovation & Project Coordination
Where Oraanj is appointed for wider delivery, the agreed renovation and project stages are coordinated in London while you remain overseas.
08 — Installation & Handover
For turnkey projects, furniture and finishing elements can be coordinated towards the end of the project before the property is prepared for handover.

Commercially, that's the wrong signal. Someone potentially appointing you to manage a London residence remotely shouldn't land on the page and immediately be asked whether they want a £600 room package. Keep those packages on the appropriate Interior Design page.
I'd also remove the current “In-House Expertise” cards about Prime Family Residences / High-Yield Investments / Heritage Homes / Apartments / Executive Workspaces. They read as generic categories. Replace them with Who We Work With, which immediately tells an overseas visitor, “Yes, this service is for someone like me.”
The sections “How We Operate…”, “Your Remote Project Roadmap”, “Breadth of Services”, “Your Interior Design Team in London” and “Challenges of Managing a London Build from Abroad” are all saying versions of the same thing. Consolidating them will make the page dramatically shorter and stronger.
There is also a copy error near the bottom:

on an international interior design page. That absolutely needs removing—it makes the content look templated.
And I would remove unsupported absolutes such as “complete financial transparency,” “safeguarding you from unexpected costs,” “without you needing to take a single flight,” “avoid every single one of these pitfalls,” “zero guesswork,” and “exactly as planned.” They're not necessary to sell the service.

 /international/uae/
 /international/saudi-arabia/
But each page must contain real region-specific information and evidence—project delivery model, local vs London responsibilities, FF&E/shipping approach, genuine projects/case studies, and how appointments work in that country. Don't simply clone this page and change “Qatar” to “Dubai.” Google recommends explicit locale structures for genuinely regional content and warns that similar regional variants need to be handled properly.
If you eventually publish Arabic versions, use separate URLs and properly implemented hreflang rather than dynamically swapping the page according to the visitor's IP. Google specifically recommends separate locale URLs and says locale-adaptive pages can be harder to crawl completely.
For this page, though, I'd keep it focused on the highest-value proposition: international clients who need a trusted London team to design, source, renovate and coordinate their property while they are abroad. That is commercially much stronger and much easier for Google and AI search to understand.
