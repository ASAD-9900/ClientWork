Designed Around Your Business
A commercial interior needs to do more than look good.
It needs to support the way the business actually operates.
Before developing the design, we look at how customers enter and move through the space, how employees work, what needs to be stored, where different activities take place and what impression the environment should create.
For a restaurant, that might mean balancing customer seating with circulation and service.
For an office, it could mean creating the right mix of focused work, meetings and collaboration.
For a customer-facing business, the entrance, reception and overall experience may be particularly important.
Understanding these requirements first allows the design to respond to the business rather than simply decorating the premises.

Customers, Staff and Brand
Three Things Every Commercial Interior Needs to Consider
Your Customers
What do people experience when they enter?
The layout, lighting, materials, furniture and circulation all influence how easy the space is to understand and how the business is perceived.
Your Team
A beautiful space can still fail if employees cannot work comfortably within it.
We consider movement, storage, work areas, collaboration and the practical requirements of the people using the premises every day.
Your Brand
The interior should feel connected to the business.
That doesn't mean covering every wall with logos or brand colours. Often the strongest brand expression comes through materials, details, atmosphere and the way the space has been designed.
The most successful commercial interiors bring all three together.

Making Better Use of Commercial Space
Every Square Metre Has a Job
Commercial property is valuable, particularly in London.
Poorly planned circulation, oversized waiting areas, unused corners or badly positioned furniture can reduce how effectively a space performs.

Customer and staff circulation

Meeting and collaboration areas
Front-of-house and back-of-house relationships
Storage requirements
Reception and waiting areas
Furniture dimensions
Accessibility considerations
Future flexibility
The objective isn't simply to fit more into the premises. It is to make the available space work better.

How Much Does Commercial Interior Design Cost in London?
Commercial interior design fees depend on the size of the premises, type of business and level of service required.
A small office requiring space planning and interior design will have a different scope from a restaurant or larger workplace involving detailed technical design, bespoke joinery, procurement and fit-out.
The existing condition of the premises and the level of alteration required can also affect the wider project budget.
We therefore start by understanding the space and what the business needs before recommending the appropriate design scope.

How Long Does a Commercial Interior Design Project Take?
The programme depends on the size and complexity of the project.
The design stage may include space planning, concept development, approvals, technical design and procurement planning before any fit-out begins.
Where physical works are required, the overall programme may also need to consider landlord requirements, statutory approvals, specialist consultants, bespoke manufacturing, furniture lead times and installation.
If you have a target opening, relocation or handover date, tell us at the beginning so it can be considered when planning the project.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does a commercial interior designer do?
A commercial interior designer plans and develops spaces used by businesses. This can include space planning, customer and staff circulation, interior design, lighting, materials, furniture, bespoke joinery and technical design depending on the project.
What types of commercial interiors does Oraanj design?
We work across offices, workplaces, restaurants, cafés, retail, hospitality and other professional or customer-facing interiors.
How much does commercial interior design cost in London?
The cost depends on the size of the premises, business type and level of design support required. We establish the project scope before recommending the appropriate service and fee.
Can Oraanj design and fit out my commercial space?
Yes. Where required, our wider Design & Build service can extend beyond interior design into refurbishment, fit-out, procurement and project management.
Can you work with my existing contractor?
Yes. Oraanj can provide the agreed design and technical information for a project that will be implemented by your chosen contractor.
Do you design offices?
Yes. Office and workplace projects can include space planning, reception areas, meeting rooms, workspaces, breakout areas, storage, furniture and lighting.
Do you design restaurants and cafés?
Yes. We design restaurant and café interiors around customer experience, seating, circulation, service requirements, lighting and the overall atmosphere.
Can you help with commercial space planning?
Yes. Space planning is an important part of our commercial interior design service and considers how customers, employees, furniture and different business activities use the premises.
Can you help with bespoke counters, reception desks and storage?
Yes. Bespoke joinery can form part of the commercial design scope.
When should I appoint an interior designer?
Ideally, involve the designer before the fit-out begins. This gives more time to resolve layouts, materials, lighting, joinery and other design decisions before they reach site.
