Start With How You Serve Coffee
The Business Model Changes the Design
Before deciding what the café should look like, we need to understand how it needs to work.
A neighbourhood coffee shop where customers stay for an hour needs a different layout from a busy commuter café serving hundreds of takeaway drinks.
A bakery café displaying fresh products needs something different again.
We start with questions such as:
How do customers order?
At the counter, table or digitally?
How much of your trade is takeaway?
A busy takeaway business needs a clear route from entrance to order, collection and exit.
What are you serving?
Coffee only? Pastries? Cakes? Hot food? Brunch?
The menu can influence counter space, display, storage and specialist equipment requirements.
How many customers do you want to seat?
We consider seating capacity alongside comfort, circulation and service.
How long do customers typically stay?
This can influence seating types, table sizes, charging points and the overall atmosphere.
What happens during your busiest hour?
The café needs to work when the queue is longest, not only when it is quiet.

The Café Counter Is the Heart of the Space
Design the Workflow Before the Counter
The counter is often where customers order, pay, collect drinks and interact with your team.
It can also contain or coordinate coffee equipment, displays, storage and service functions.
That makes its layout one of the most important design decisions in the café.
We consider:
ORDER
Where does the customer approach and place the order?
PAY
Can payment happen without blocking the next customer?
PREPARE
How does the barista move between the coffee machine, grinder, milk, sink and other equipment?
COLLECT
Where do customers wait for drinks without blocking the ordering queue?
DISPLAY
Where should pastries, cakes or retail products sit within the customer journey?
STORE
What needs to be accessible to staff during service?
Once the workflow is understood, we can develop the counter around the operation as well as the design.

Priorities may include:

Different table sizes
Longer customer dwell time
Lighting and atmosphere
Accessible power where appropriate
Good circulation between tables
Takeaway + Sit-In
The challenge is preventing one customer journey from disrupting the other.
A good layout allows takeaway customers to order and collect efficiently while seated customers can enjoy the café without feeling surrounded by a queue.

What Should You Budget for a Café Fit-Out?
Look Beyond the Cost of the Furniture
There is no sensible single cost for designing and fitting out a café without understanding the premises.
The budget can be affected by:
EXISTING CONDITION
Taking over a functioning café can be very different from starting with a shell unit.
SIZE
Floor area influences materials, furniture and the extent of physical works.
COUNTER
A bespoke coffee counter can be a significant element of both the design and fit-out.
EQUIPMENT
Coffee machines, refrigeration, food preparation and other specialist equipment may affect the wider design and services.
JOINERY
Banquette seating, counters, displays and storage can increase the amount of bespoke work.
SERVICES
The existing and proposed electrical, plumbing, ventilation and other requirements can affect the wider project.
FINISHES
Flooring, lighting, wall finishes, furniture and specification level all influence cost.
PROFESSIONAL & APPROVAL REQUIREMENTS
Depending on the property and works, other consultants, permissions or approvals may also need to be considered.
We recommend establishing the business requirements, premises and design scope before setting a meaningful project budget.

A Café Design Should Work at 8:30am
Not Just in a 3D Render
A café can look fantastic when it is empty.
The real test comes during the morning rush.
Imagine:
Twelve customers arrive within five minutes.
Four want takeaway coffee.
Two are waiting for drinks.
Someone is choosing pastries.
A delivery arrives.
A customer is looking for a table.
Staff are moving between the coffee machine, till and service area.
Can everyone move without getting in each other's way?
That's why we consider the operational side of the café alongside the interior.
The finished design should look good when photographed — but more importantly, it should make sense when the café is busy.
