Start With How the Bar Will Operate
The Business Model Changes the Design
A neighbourhood wine bar needs a different layout from a busy cocktail venue.
A hotel lounge operates differently from a late-night bar.
A gastro pub serving food needs something different again.
Before developing the look of the venue, we need to understand how the business works.
How many customers are you planning for?
Is the venue mainly seated or standing?
Will you serve food?
How many bartenders need to work behind the counter?
Is there table service?
How much refrigeration is required?
Where will glassware be stored?
How will deliveries reach the bar?
Where will empty bottles and waste go?
What happens during your busiest hour?
The answers influence the layout long before colours and finishes are selected.

Lighting Changes the Bar After Dark
Design for the Hours You Actually Trade
A bar may look completely different at 3pm and 10pm.
Lighting can help create that transition.
Depending on the venue, the scheme may combine:
AMBIENT LIGHTING
The overall level of illumination within the space.
BAR LIGHTING
Lighting integrated around the front or back bar.
TABLE LIGHTING
Creating more intimate areas around customer seating.
ACCENT LIGHTING
Drawing attention to bottles, artwork, materials or architectural features.
DECORATIVE LIGHTING
Pendants, wall lights and feature fittings that contribute to the identity of the venue.
OPERATIONAL LIGHTING
Practical lighting where staff need to work safely and effectively.
The lighting strategy should be developed alongside the layout and interior rather than added at the end.

Bar Interior Design Questions
What does a bar interior designer do?
A bar interior designer considers the layout, bar counter, customer circulation, seating, staff workflow, lighting, materials, furniture and overall atmosphere of the venue.
Can you design the bar counter?
Yes. Bespoke front and back bar design can form part of the agreed interior design and joinery scope.
Can you help us maximise seating?
Yes. We can explore different seating arrangements while considering circulation, customer comfort, staff movement and the wider requirements of the venue.
Can you design a cocktail bar?
Yes. Oraanj can design cocktail bars, lounges and other selected hospitality interiors.
Can you refurbish an existing bar?
Yes. We can review the existing venue and discuss whether elements should be retained, updated or completely redesigned.
Do you design pubs?
Yes. Pub and gastro-pub interiors can be discussed depending on the project scope.
Can you provide bar design and fit-out?
Yes. Oraanj can provide interior design or discuss a wider service continuing into refurbishment, fit-out and project management.
Can you source the furniture and lighting?
Yes. Furniture, decorative lighting and other agreed FF&E can form part of the sourcing and procurement service.
Do you handle licensing?
Licensing and other specialist statutory matters may require separate appropriately qualified advisers or consultants. We can develop the interior design alongside the relevant project information and project team.
When should I contact a bar interior designer?
Ideally before the layout, bar counter, equipment and major fit-out decisions are fixed. If you are considering a new premises, early design input can also help you understand how the available space might work for your concept.

Remove the packages completely
I would definitely remove the £600, £900 and £1,500 packages and the entire comparison table from this page. They're currently one of the first things visitors encounter.
A bar is not a “room/zone” in the same sense as a bedroom.
A serious bar enquiry could involve counter design, back bar, equipment coordination, customer flow, furniture, lighting, joinery, toilets, technical design and fit-out. You want the client to send the floor plan + venue information + budget + opening date so you can scope it properly.
Remove claims about increasing revenue/profitability
Your current page says the design encourages customers to “stay longer, spend more and return again”, refers to ensuring “maximum profitability”, and promises a “measurable return on your commercial investment.”
I would remove those claims.
Interior design can absolutely support customer experience and operations, but you can't attribute a client's profitability or spending directly to the interior without evidence.
A stronger line is:
We design around customer experience and the way the venue needs to operate.
It sounds credible rather than salesy.
Make the bar counter the hero of the page
For Café, we made the coffee counter/workflow central. For Bar, I'd go even further.
