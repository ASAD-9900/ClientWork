How Many Desks Do You Actually Need?
Start With Headcount, Not Furniture
One of the first questions in office space planning is not:
“Which desks should we buy?”
It is:
“How many people will actually use the office at the same time?”
Imagine a company with 60 employees.
If 60 people attend every day, its workplace requirements are very different from a hybrid business where perhaps 35–40 people are normally present at one time.
But reducing desks isn't automatically the answer.
The released space may need to become:
Meeting rooms
Focus rooms
Video-call booths
Collaboration areas
Breakout space
Informal meeting areas
Storage
Client-facing space
We therefore look at occupancy and working patterns before developing the floor plan.

Before You Sign an Office Lease
Test the Space Before You Commit to the Layout
A floor area on an estate agent's brochure doesn't tell you whether the premises will work well for your business.
Two offices with the same square footage can perform very differently because of their shape, columns, windows, entrances, circulation and existing services.
Before developing the full design, useful questions include:
How many workstations could realistically fit?
How many meeting rooms do we need?
Where will visitors arrive?
Do teams need to sit together?
Where can private calls happen?
How much storage is required?
How does natural light affect the layout?
Can the space accommodate future growth?
What existing elements could potentially be retained?
What landlord or building-management requirements need to be investigated?
Where appropriate to our appointment, Oraanj can help assess the interior space-planning potential of a proposed office.
Lease terms, fire strategy, building regulations, structural matters, M&E and other specialist requirements should be reviewed by the relevant professional advisers where required.

Designing for Hybrid Working
Fewer Desks Doesn't Automatically Mean a Better Office
Hybrid working has changed what many businesses need from their workplace.
If employees are travelling into the office primarily to collaborate, meet clients or work with colleagues, rows of individual desks may no longer be the best use of the floor area.
But removing desks without understanding attendance patterns can create a different problem.
We look at the balance between:
Individual Work
Workstations and areas where employees can concentrate.
Collaboration
Spaces for teams to work together without disrupting focused areas.
Meetings
Rooms of different sizes rather than relying only on one large boardroom.
Video Calls
Smaller spaces where calls can happen without filling meeting rooms or disturbing colleagues.
Breakout
Informal areas for conversation, short meetings and time away from the desk.
Social Space
Where appropriate, spaces that encourage people to spend time together beyond formal meetings.
The right balance depends on how your particular organisation works.

Acoustics, Lighting and Privacy
The Things You Notice Once the Office Is Busy
A workplace can look excellent when it is empty.
The real test is what happens when people arrive.
ACOUSTICS
Open workplaces can become distracting when conversations, calls and meetings overlap.
The layout and material strategy can consider the relationship between quieter and more active areas, with specialist acoustic advice obtained where required.
LIGHTING
Different workplace activities can require different lighting conditions.
We consider natural light, general illumination, meeting spaces, feature lighting and the overall atmosphere as part of the interior design.
PRIVACY
Not every conversation belongs in an open-plan environment.
Meeting rooms, focus spaces, screens and the positioning of different teams can help create a better balance between openness and privacy.
These decisions need to be considered during planning rather than added after the furniture arrives.

Refurbishing an Existing Office?
You May Not Need to Replace Everything
An office redesign doesn't necessarily require a complete strip-out.
Depending on the condition of the workplace, some existing elements may still work well.
We can consider whether elements such as furniture, flooring, lighting, meeting rooms, storage or joinery could potentially be retained and incorporated into the new design.
The redesign can then focus investment on the parts of the office that most need to change.
This can be particularly useful where the business needs a new layout or identity without rebuilding the entire workplace.

Office Interior Design Questions
What does an office interior designer do?
An office interior designer plans the workplace around how the organisation uses it. This can include space planning, workstation layouts, meeting rooms, reception, collaboration areas, lighting, materials, furniture, bespoke joinery and technical design depending on the project.
How many desks can fit in my office?
This depends on the floor area, shape of the premises, furniture dimensions, circulation, meeting-room requirements and relevant regulatory considerations. Space planning allows different layouts to be tested against your actual workplace requirements.
Can you help us plan a hybrid office?
Yes. We can look at typical attendance, desk requirements, collaboration, meetings, calls and other activities to develop an appropriate workplace layout.
Can Oraanj design our office before we sign the lease?
Where appropriate to the appointment, we can help assess the interior space-planning potential of proposed premises. Legal, lease and specialist technical matters should be reviewed by the relevant professional advisers.
Can you refurbish an existing office?
Yes. The project can range from layout and interior-design changes to a wider office refurbishment or fit-out.
Can we keep some of our existing furniture?
Yes. Existing furniture can be reviewed and incorporated where it suits the new layout and design.
Do you design reception areas and boardrooms?
Yes. Reception areas, meeting rooms, boardrooms, executive offices and other client-facing spaces can form part of the wider office design.
Can you provide office furniture?
Furniture specification and procurement can be included depending on the agreed project scope.
Can Oraanj manage the office fit-out?
Yes. For clients requiring wider delivery, Oraanj can discuss refurbishment, fit-out, procurement and project management.
When should we appoint an office interior designer?
Ideally, allow enough time before your intended move or refurbishment for space planning, design development, technical information, approvals, procurement and fit-out to be considered properly.
