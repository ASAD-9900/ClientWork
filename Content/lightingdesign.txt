What Is Layered Lighting?
One Room Usually Needs More Than One Type of Light
A well-planned interior often combines different layers of lighting rather than relying on one ceiling light.
AMBIENT LIGHTING
Provides the general level of light within the room.
This might include ceiling lights, indirect lighting or other sources used to create overall illumination.
TASK LIGHTING
Provides more focused light where something specific happens.
For example:
Kitchen preparation
Reading
Working at a desk
Applying make-up
Getting dressed
ACCENT LIGHTING
Draws attention to particular parts of the interior.
This may include:
Artwork
Shelving
Joinery
Architectural details
Textured walls
DECORATIVE LIGHTING
Lighting that becomes part of the visual design itself.
For example:
Pendants
Wall lights
Table lamps
Floor lamps
A successful scheme considers how these different layers work together.

Lighting Should Follow the Furniture
Dining Table + Pendant
A pendant should relate to the position and dimensions of the dining table rather than simply being centred within the room.
If the table moves significantly later, the lighting may no longer feel properly positioned.
Bed + Bedside Lighting
Bedside pendants or wall lights need to relate to:
Bed width
Headboard
Side tables
Switch positions
How the client uses the room
Sofa + Reading Light
Floor lamps, wall lights and other focused lighting should be considered alongside the furniture arrangement.
Kitchen Island + Pendants
Pendant positions need to consider the dimensions and position of the island as well as the wider kitchen layout.
Artwork + Accent Lighting
If important artwork is planned, the lighting strategy should consider where it will be displayed.
This is why we prefer to develop lighting after the main space plan is understood but before electrical work is finalised.

Warm or Cool Light?
Colour Temperature Changes How an Interior Feels
Not all white light looks the same.
Some light appears warmer and softer. Other light appears cooler and crisper.
The appropriate choice depends on the room, materials, use of the space and overall design.
A warm interior with timber, fabrics and soft finishes may respond differently to light from a highly functional workspace.
Consistency also matters.
Using noticeably different colour temperatures without a clear reason can make an interior feel disconnected.
The lighting should therefore be considered alongside the materials rather than selected in isolation.

Dining lighting
Living-area lighting
Joinery lighting
Decorative lighting
This can make it easier to move from practical daytime use to a softer evening atmosphere.
Smart-lighting requirements can also be discussed where relevant to the project, with specialist technical design and installation undertaken by the appropriate professionals.

When Should Lighting Be Designed?
Before Electrical Positions Are Finalised
The best time to plan lighting is after you understand the main layout but before relevant electrical work has progressed too far.
For a renovation, the sequence might look like:
SPACE PLANNING
↓
FURNITURE LAYOUT
↓
KITCHEN & JOINERY PLANNING
↓
LIGHTING DESIGN
↓
ELECTRICAL COORDINATION
↓
CONSTRUCTION
↓

↓

Leaving lighting until late in the project can make it harder to coordinate positions with the rest of the interior.

Lighting Design Questions
What does a lighting designer do?
A lighting designer considers where and how light should be used within a space. This can include ambient, task, accent and decorative lighting as well as the relationship between lighting, furniture, architecture and the way the room is used.
When should I plan lighting during a renovation?
Ideally after the main layout and furniture positions are understood but before relevant electrical positions and construction work are finalised.
Can I use my own electrician?
Yes. Oraanj can develop the interior lighting design while your chosen electrician undertakes the relevant electrical work, testing and certification.
Do you design lighting for one room?
Yes. Lighting design can be discussed for individual rooms as well as complete properties, depending on the project.
Can you help choose decorative lights?
Yes. Pendants, wall lights and other decorative lighting can form part of the agreed lighting design scope.
Do you design kitchen lighting?
Yes. Kitchen lighting can be developed around cabinetry, work surfaces, islands, dining areas and the wider room layout.
Can you design lighting inside wardrobes and joinery?
Yes. Integrated joinery lighting can be considered alongside the bespoke furniture design.
Can you plan dimmers and lighting zones?
The intended zoning and control strategy can be considered as part of the interior lighting design. Specialist electrical design and installation should be undertaken by the appropriate qualified professionals.
Do you provide lighting design for commercial interiors?
Yes. Lighting can form part of Oraanj's office, restaurant, café and other selected commercial interior projects.
Is lighting design included in your wider interior design service?
It can be included depending on the package and agreed project scope.
