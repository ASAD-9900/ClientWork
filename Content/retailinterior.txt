Start With How Your Store Needs to Work
The Business Comes Before the Decoration
A fashion boutique works differently from a beauty store.
A showroom displaying a small number of high-value products needs a different layout from a busy shop carrying hundreds of items.
Before deciding how the interior should look, we need to understand how customers and staff will actually use it.
We start with questions such as:
What are you selling?
How much product needs to be displayed and how much needs to be stored?
How do customers shop?
Do they browse independently, need assistance or move towards a consultation area?
What should customers notice first?
Your entrance and initial sightline can influence how the rest of the shop is experienced.
How often do displays change?
Seasonal collections and changing stock may require flexible display systems.
What does your team need?
Storage, tills, packing areas, fitting rooms and staff circulation all need to work behind the customer experience.

Plan the Customer Journey
What Happens After Someone Walks Through the Door?
Retail space planning is about more than fitting display units into a room.
We consider how customers enter, what they see first, how they move through the space and where important products or services should sit.
Depending on the business, this may include:
Entrance & Window Display
Creating a strong first impression while maintaining visibility into the store.
Customer Circulation
Planning comfortable routes without making the interior feel empty or overcrowded.
Product Display
Positioning products so they can be seen, reached and explored naturally.
Feature Areas
Creating focal points for new collections, hero products or important brand moments.
Consultation & Service Areas
Planning spaces where customers can sit, discuss products or receive a service.
Till & Checkout
Considering customer queues, staff access, packing and storage.
Back-of-House Storage
Making sure stock and operational requirements are considered without compromising the customer-facing interior.

Make the Product the Focus
Design Around What You're Selling
The interior should support the product rather than compete with it.
A jewellery showroom may need controlled lighting, secure displays and a quieter customer journey.
A fashion boutique may need flexible rails, folded displays, mirrors and fitting rooms.
A beauty store may require illuminated displays, consultation areas and easy access to products.
We develop the interior around your merchandise, customer and brand rather than applying the same design formula to every shop.

Larger products often require a different approach to circulation, sightlines and room-style displays.
Independent Shops

Flagship & Brand Spaces
The interior can provide greater opportunity for storytelling, feature installations and a more immersive expression of the brand.

Retail Interior Design FAQs
What does a retail interior designer do?
A retail interior designer plans how a shop should function and feel. This can include customer circulation, product displays, furniture, lighting, materials, bespoke joinery, storage and the overall design of the customer-facing space.
Can you design a shop from an empty retail unit?
Yes. We can work with empty units as well as existing stores being refurbished, depending on the project requirements.
Can you help with the shop layout?
Yes. Space planning can consider customer movement, product displays, tills, fitting or consultation areas, storage and staff circulation.
Do you design bespoke retail displays?
Yes. Where required, we can design bespoke displays, shelving, counters, storage and other fitted interior elements around the products and space.
Do you provide 3D visuals?
3D visualisation can be included within the agreed design scope to help you understand the proposed store before fit-out begins.
Can Oraanj manage the retail fit-out?
Yes, depending on the project. Oraanj can provide interior design as a standalone service or discuss a wider Design & Build scope covering refurbishment, fit-out and project management.
Can you work with my existing shopfitter or contractor?
Yes. If you already have a contractor, we can discuss the appropriate interior design and technical design scope around your existing delivery team.
How much does retail interior design cost?
Fees depend on the size of the store, design requirements, technical scope and level of service. Retail projects are scoped individually rather than using standard residential per-room packages.
When should I appoint a retail interior designer?
Ideally, involve the designer before major fit-out decisions or orders are made. Early planning gives more opportunity to resolve the layout, displays, lighting and material decisions before physical work begins.
What do you need before the first consultation?
If available, send us the store location, floor plan, photographs, brand information, products being sold, approximate budget and target opening date.
