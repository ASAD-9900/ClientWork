What Is a 3D Interior Render?
A Realistic View of a Proposed Interior
A floor plan tells you where things go.
A 3D render helps you understand what the proposed interior could actually look like.
It brings different design decisions together in one visual, including:
Furniture
Materials
Colour
Flooring
Wall Finishes
Lighting
Bespoke Joinery
Kitchens & Bathrooms
Accessories
Room Proportions
Instead of trying to imagine the finished room from separate samples, drawings and product images, you can see how the different elements relate to one another.

Why Use 3D Visualisation?
Make Important Decisions Before Work Begins
Some design decisions are difficult to judge from a floor plan alone.
A material may look beautiful as a sample but feel very different when used across an entire room.
A media wall may work on an elevation but feel too dominant once seen in context.
A sofa may technically fit but appear too large for the space.
3D visualisation allows these decisions to be reviewed together.
It can help you consider:
Does the furniture feel correctly proportioned?
Do the materials work together?
Is the room becoming too busy?
Does the lighting complement the design?
Is the bespoke joinery appropriately scaled?
Does the colour palette work across the complete room?
Is this actually how I want the finished interior to feel?
The purpose isn't simply to create an attractive image.
It's to help you make better-informed design decisions.

From Render to Reality
How Close Can a 3D Render Be to the Finished Interior?
A well-developed render can communicate the intended layout, proportions, materials, furniture and overall character of an interior very clearly.
However, the final room may not be completely identical.
During a real project, changes can happen because of:
Physical material samples
Product availability
Site conditions
Supplier lead times
Client decisions
Technical requirements
Approved design changes
The render should therefore communicate the intended design direction rather than be treated as a guarantee that every physical detail will appear exactly the same.

3D Rendering or Interior Design?
They Are Not the Same Service

“I need someone to develop what the room should look like.”
Interior design involves making the wider decisions around layout, furniture, materials, colour, lighting and the overall character of the space.
If you already have the design information required, a standalone 3D visualisation service may be appropriate.

3D Rendering or Technical Drawings?
One Shows the Design. The Other Communicates Detail.
A 3D render is primarily visual.
It helps you understand how the proposed interior could look and feel.
Technical drawings communicate more precise information such as dimensions, positions, elevations and agreed construction-related interior details.
For projects progressing into physical works, you may require both.

Can a Builder Work From a 3D Render?
A Render Is Not Automatically a Construction Drawing
A 3D visual can communicate the intended appearance of an interior very effectively.
However, it does not automatically contain all of the technical information required to build it.
For example:
A bespoke media wall may require detailed elevations and dimensions.
Lighting may require appropriate plans and electrical coordination.
Custom furniture may require fabrication drawings.
Other elements may require specifications or specialist information.
If your project is progressing into renovation, we can advise which additional interior design information may be appropriate.

3D Rendering Questions
What is a 3D interior render?
A 3D interior render is a computer-generated visual representation of a proposed interior. It can show furniture, materials, lighting, colour, joinery and the wider design direction before the physical interior is completed.
Can you create a 3D render from a floor plan?
Yes, depending on the information available. Floor plans, dimensions, photographs and other project information can be used to develop a 3D interior visual.
Can I book 3D rendering without full interior design?
Potentially, yes. If you already have a sufficiently developed design and the necessary information, Oraanj can discuss a standalone 3D visualisation service.
What information do you need?
Depending on the project, we may need floor plans, dimensions, photographs, design references, furniture information, materials and other relevant design information.
Can you create 3D renders for one room?
Yes. 3D rendering can be used for an individual room or multiple spaces depending on the project.
Can you create 3D renders for an entire house?
Yes. Multiple rooms can be visualised as part of a wider residential interior design project.
Do you create commercial 3D renders?
Yes. Oraanj can provide 3D interior visualisation for selected office, restaurant, café, bar and retail projects.
Can you show different furniture or materials?
Where this forms part of the agreed design and revision scope, alternative design elements can be developed and reviewed.
Can changes be made after I see the render?
Revisions can be made according to the scope and number of revisions agreed for the service.
Is a 3D render exactly how the finished room will look?
A render communicates the intended design direction. Physical materials, product availability, site conditions and approved changes during implementation can result in differences in the completed interior.
Can builders build directly from a 3D render?
A 3D render should not automatically be treated as a construction drawing. Technical drawings, dimensions, specifications or specialist information may also be required depending on what is being built.
Can Oraanj take the design into construction?
For suitable projects, Oraanj can continue into interior design, technical development, procurement, renovation and Design & Build according to the agreed scope.
