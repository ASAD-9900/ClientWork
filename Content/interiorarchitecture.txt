What Is Interior Architecture?
The Planning Between an Existing Property and the Finished Interior
Interior architecture focuses on how the inside of a property is organised, planned and developed.
It sits between the existing building and the finished interior.
Depending on the project, this can involve:
Space planning
Room layouts
Internal reconfiguration
Furniture planning
Circulation
Lighting layouts
Electrical layouts
Kitchens and bathrooms
Bespoke joinery
Wall elevations
Materials and finishes
Technical interior drawings
The aim is to resolve important spatial and design decisions before they become urgent questions during construction.

When Do You Need Interior Architecture?
You're Changing the Layout
If rooms are being opened up, divided or used differently, the new arrangement should be tested before construction begins.
You're Renovating the Whole Property
A complete renovation requires decisions about more than finishes. Layouts, lighting, electrical points, kitchens, bathrooms, joinery and furniture all need to work together.
Your Current Layout Doesn't Work
Sometimes the property has enough floor area but uses it badly.
Better circulation, furniture planning or storage can make a significant difference without necessarily increasing the size of the property.
You've Just Bought a Property
Before starting a major refurbishment, interior architecture can help you understand how the existing property could be adapted around the way you want to live.
Your Builder Needs Drawings
If the design has only been discussed verbally or through inspiration images, additional drawings may be required to communicate the intended interior more clearly.
You're Planning Bespoke Joinery
Wardrobes, media walls, shelving and other fitted furniture need to relate accurately to the room, surrounding architecture, lighting and electrical positions.

Start With the Existing Floor Plan
Then Ask What Could Work Better
One of the most useful parts of interior architecture is testing different ways of using the same property.
Imagine an existing ground floor with:
A separate kitchen
A dining room that is rarely used
A living room disconnected from the kitchen
Limited storage
Poor circulation
There may be several possible solutions.
OPTION 01 — OPEN IT UP
Create a more connected kitchen, dining and living arrangement.
OPTION 02 — KEEP SOME SEPARATION
Improve the relationship between rooms while retaining quieter or more formal spaces.
OPTION 03 — REPLAN THE FUNCTIONS
Move particular uses to different areas of the property to make better use of light, circulation or available space.
There isn't automatically one correct answer.
We compare the options against the property and the way the client wants to use it.

The Details Need to Work Together
A Few Centimetres Can Matter
Interior architecture is often about relationships between different elements.
DINING TABLE + PENDANT
The light should relate to where the table will actually sit.
BED + SOCKETS
Bedside electrical positions need to relate to the bed, headboard and side tables.
MEDIA WALL + ELECTRICAL
The television, equipment, storage, power and cable routes need to be considered together.
WARDROBE + LIGHTING
A wardrobe can affect lighting positions, sockets, switches and circulation.
KITCHEN + DINING
Kitchen cabinetry, island dimensions and dining furniture influence how people move through the space.
FLOORING + DOORS
Different finishes and levels may affect transitions and surrounding details.
Resolving these relationships on drawings is usually easier than discovering them once installation has begun.

Interior Architecture Questions
What does an interior architect do?
Interior architecture focuses on planning and developing the internal space. Depending on the project, this can include layouts, circulation, lighting, electrical positions, kitchens, bathrooms, bespoke joinery and technical interior drawings.
What is the difference between interior architecture and interior design?
Interior architecture generally involves more detailed spatial and technical planning. Interior design focuses more heavily on materials, furniture, colour, finishes and the overall character of the interior. Many renovation projects require elements of both.
Do I need interior architecture before renovating?
Not every renovation requires the same level of technical design. It is particularly useful where layouts are changing or where lighting, electrical positions, kitchens, bathrooms and bespoke joinery need to be coordinated before work begins.
Can Oraanj redesign my floor plan?
Yes. Space planning and internal layout development can form part of our interior architecture service, subject to the existing property and relevant specialist requirements.
Can you provide space planning only?
Yes. Where a full interior architecture service isn't required, space planning can be discussed separately.
Can you produce drawings for my builder?
Yes, depending on the project and agreed scope. Oraanj can prepare relevant interior design drawings and information for use by your contractor.
Do your drawings replace structural or engineering drawings?
No. Structural, engineering, specialist M&E, fire, regulatory and other professional information should be prepared by appropriately qualified specialists where required.
Can you design lighting and electrical layouts?
We can develop the interior design intent and proposed positions in relation to the layout. Specialist technical design, compliance and installation should be undertaken by the appropriate qualified professionals where required.
Can Oraanj design bespoke joinery?
Yes. Wardrobes, media walls, storage, cabinetry and other fitted elements can form part of the interior architecture service.
Can you also manage the renovation?
Yes. If you would prefer wider support after the design stage, Oraanj can discuss Design & Build, renovation and project-management services.
