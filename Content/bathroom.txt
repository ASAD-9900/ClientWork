Bath or Walk-In Shower?
Start With How You Actually Use the Bathroom
Not every bathroom needs both.
A bath may be important for:
Families with young children
Clients who regularly use a bath
Certain main family bathrooms
A larger walk-in shower may make more sense where:
The bath is rarely used
The existing shower feels cramped
Ease of access is important
The room would benefit from a more open layout
Where space permits, both may be possible.
The right answer depends on the household, available space and the role of that particular bathroom.

Planning a Small Bathroom
Don't Try to Fit Everything In
A small bathroom can work extremely well when unnecessary elements are removed and the important ones are planned properly.
We consider:
Door position
Shower dimensions
Basin size
Toilet position
Storage
Mirror size
Lighting
Tile scale
Sightlines
Clear floor area
Sometimes one larger, well-planned shower is better than trying to squeeze in both a small bath and a small shower.
The aim is not to fit the maximum number of things into the room.
It's to make the room easier to use.

Choosing Bathroom Tiles
Don't Choose Them in Isolation
The same tile can feel very different depending on:
Room size
Natural light
Artificial lighting
Grout colour
Tile direction
Brassware finish
Vanity colour
Flooring
How much of the room is tiled
We consider the tiles alongside the complete material palette.
Depending on the design, using fewer materials more consistently can sometimes create a calmer result than introducing too many different finishes.

Large Tiles or Small Tiles?
Both Can Work
There isn't one tile size that works for every bathroom.
Larger tiles can create a quieter visual effect with fewer grout lines.
Smaller tiles may work particularly well for:
Details
Niches
Feature areas
Certain shower floors
Curved or unusually shaped areas
The right choice depends on the room dimensions, tile format, design and technical requirements.
The setting-out should be considered before installation so awkward cuts and alignments can be identified early.

Moving the Toilet, Shower or Basin
Sometimes Possible. Always Plan It Properly.
Changing bathroom positions can sometimes improve the room considerably.
But moving sanitaryware can affect:
Soil and waste pipes
Drainage falls
Water supplies
Floor construction
Wall build-ups
Ventilation
Electrical positions
Waterproofing
Access for future maintenance
The feasibility depends on the individual property and existing services.
We therefore develop the interior proposal alongside the relevant technical information rather than assuming every item can simply be moved anywhere.

Waterproofing Matters
The Important Parts Aren't All Visible
Some of the most important work in a bathroom disappears behind the finished surfaces.
Depending on the project, this can include:
Substrate preparation
Waterproofing systems
Drainage
Pipework
Ventilation
Electrical provisions
Falls within shower areas
Access for maintenance
The visible design should therefore be developed alongside the practical requirements of the installation.
Appropriate waterproofing, plumbing and electrical work should be undertaken by competent professionals in accordance with the requirements applicable to the project.

Ventilation
A Beautiful Bathroom Still Needs to Manage Moisture
Bathrooms create humidity.
Ventilation should therefore be considered as part of the renovation rather than as an afterthought.
The appropriate solution depends on the property, bathroom location and existing ventilation arrangements.
Where specialist design, calculations or compliance are required, these should be undertaken by the appropriate qualified professionals.

Bathroom Renovation Questions
What does a bathroom renovation include?
It depends on the agreed scope. A complete renovation may include strip-out, layout changes, plumbing, electrical work, waterproofing, tiling, sanitaryware, brassware, lighting, storage, decoration and installation.
Can Oraanj design and renovate my bathroom?
Yes. You can appoint Oraanj for bathroom design or discuss a wider service continuing into renovation and project delivery.
Can I move the toilet or shower?
Potentially. The feasibility depends on drainage, soil pipes, water supplies, floor construction and the existing property. These conditions need to be reviewed before confirming the layout.
Can you replace my bath with a walk-in shower?
Yes, where the room and relevant technical conditions allow. We would consider whether the change improves the bathroom for the way you use it.
Can you design a small bathroom?
Yes. Small bathrooms particularly benefit from careful layout, appropriately sized fittings, storage and lighting.
Can you design a wet room?
Yes. Wet-room design can be developed as part of a bathroom project, with appropriate specialist consideration given to drainage, falls, waterproofing and installation.
Can you design bespoke bathroom storage?
Yes. Custom vanities and storage can be developed where required.
Do you help choose tiles and sanitaryware?
Yes. Material, sanitaryware and brassware selection can form part of the agreed bathroom design service.
Can I use my own builder?
Yes. Oraanj can potentially provide the design and agreed technical information while your chosen contractor undertakes the renovation.
When should I contact Oraanj?
Ideally before buying sanitaryware, ordering tiles or finalising plumbing and electrical positions. This gives us more opportunity to consider the bathroom as a complete design.

What I would change from the current page
The first thing I'd remove is the £600 / £900 / £1,500 packages. Your current page calls the £1,500 option a “Design & Build Pack”, even though the inclusions are essentially design, drawings and technical information.
That's particularly risky on a bathroom page. A client could reasonably interpret £1,500 Design & Build as being connected to actually building the bathroom.
Keep those general packages on the Interior Design page. For bathroom enquiries, establish the room, scope and whether they need design only or design + renovation.
I'd also remove the large “Bathroom Space Planning vs On-Site Installation” table. It's technically detailed but isn't something a homeowner needs immediately after the hero. The rewritten “What Does Oraanj Manage?” section communicates the same idea much more naturally.
One claim I would definitely change
The current page says:
“Most bathroom renovations take between 2 and 4 weeks.”
I wouldn't make that a blanket statement.
A straightforward replacement bathroom may fit within that kind of timeframe, but a more involved project with layout changes, bespoke joinery, stone, specialist products, approvals or unexpected existing conditions can take longer.
For Oraanj, I'd rather say:
The renovation programme is established after the design, specification and site requirements are understood.
It sounds professional and doesn't create an expectation you later have to defend.
