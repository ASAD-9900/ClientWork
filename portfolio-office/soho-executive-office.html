Workplace Architecture & Commercial Fit-Out

A Professional Workplace with a More Welcoming Character

This Soho office scheme combines practical working spaces with the warmth of a residential interior.

Timber joinery and illuminated display niches introduce character, while a defined reception provides a clear arrival point. Glazed divisions organise the interior without creating a visually enclosed corridor.

A kitchen island gives the breakout area its own focal point, offering a different setting from the desk. The design approach connects these spaces through coordinated finishes, lighting and furniture rather than treating each room separately.

Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions.

01 Challenge

Balancing Openness with Privacy

Transparent divisions can maintain visual connection, but some activities need greater discretion.

Design Response

Define the visual and acoustic privacy required for each room. Coordinate glazing, doors, ceilings and service routes against those requirements, with specialist assessment where needed.

02 Challenge

A partition rating alone should not be presented as proof that conversations cannot be heard.

Creating Warmth without Losing Practicality Residential-style finishes need to accommodate daily office use.

Design Response

Select furniture, upholstery and surfaces around cleaning, durability and maintenance requirements. Use timber tones and lighting to introduce warmth while retaining suitable working conditions.

03 Challenge

Integrating Equipment into Joinery

Technology can create clutter when power, cables and ventilation are considered too late.

Design Response

Confirm equipment dimensions and connection requirements before detailing cabinetry. Provide access for maintenance and appropriate ventilation where equipment is enclosed.

04 Challenge

Making the Breakout Area Useful

A kitchen island needs to support the intended workplace activities rather than serve only as a decorative feature.

Design Response

Establish whether the area is for refreshments, informal meetings, meals or a combination. Coordinate seating, appliances, storage, power and cleaning arrangements accordingly.

05 Challenge

Coordinating Lighting across Different Settings

Reception, desks, corridors and breakout areas require different lighting approaches.

Design Response

Combine appropriate task lighting with ambient and accent lighting. Check glare, screen positions and the visibility of circulation routes during detailed design.

06 Challenge

Working with Existing Building Services

New room divisions and kitchen facilities can affect ventilation, plumbing and electrical arrangements.

Design Response

Survey the premises and coordinate specialist requirements before finalising the layout. Confirm landlord interfaces and the capacity of retained services.

These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Soho Office Fit-Out Cost?

Provisional occupier fit-out allowance: £120,000–£300,000 excluding VAT.

This range is intended for a defined office fit-out in premises with suitable existing base-build services. It may include selected partitions, internal finishes, reception joinery, storage, furniture, kitchen cabinetry, lighting alterations and installation.

  • Workplace planning and design: Brief, surveys, layouts and specifications
  • Partitions and finishes: Enclosed rooms, flooring and decoration
  • Reception and display joinery: Desk, shelving, cupboards and lighting interfaces
  • Furniture: Desks, task chairs, visitor and breakout seating
  • Kitchen breakout area: Cabinetry, countertop, appliances and installation
  • Power, data and lighting: Defined alterations and connection requirements
  • Handover: Testing, snagging and agreed documentation
  • Major heating, cooling or ventilation replacement, incoming-services upgrades, structural alterations, specialist server infrastructure and substantial acoustic construction require separate assessment unless expressly included.: Lease costs, relocation, business IT infrastructure and ongoing software subscriptions are excluded. Professional fees, surveys and landlord-related charges should be identified separately.

Allow a separate contingency of 10–15%, reviewed following survey, with VAT added where applicable.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

5–9 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 5–9 months, with some stages overlapping.

  • Workplace brief, surveys and feasibility: 2–4 weeks
  • Space planning and concept design: 3–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and services coordination: 4–8 weeks
  • Landlord review and approvals: Confirm for the premises and proposed work
  • Procurement and joinery manufacture: 8–14 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 8–14 weeks
  • Testing, snagging and handover: 1–2 weeks
