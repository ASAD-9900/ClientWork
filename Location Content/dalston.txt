Plan Bedrooms Around More Than the Bed
Wardrobe access, bedside lighting, dressing space and occasional work all influence the layout. We establish what the room needs to accommodate without filling every available wall.
Combine Existing Pieces With New Selections
You do not need to replace everything to create a coherent interior. We can work around furniture, books, artwork and collected objects, introducing complementary colours, textiles and finishes.

How Can You Fit Living, Dining and Working Into a Dalston Flat?
Start by deciding which activities need permanent space and which can share it.
If you work from home every day, a comfortable workstation deserves more consideration than an occasional laptop position. If you regularly host friends, dining access and flexible seating may take priority.
We develop layouts around these choices. A compact desk could sit within fitted storage, while a dining table may work better against a wall between meals. Lighting can help each area function independently.
The aim is to reduce conflict between activities while keeping useful routes through the room clear.
Can You Update a Rented Home Without Major Alterations?
A furnishing-led scheme may offer useful improvements, subject to your tenancy and landlord’s requirements.
Furniture placement, rugs, lamps, freestanding storage and window treatments can change how a room feels without a full refurbishment.
We consider pieces that suit the current property and may remain useful if you move. Where fixing, decoration or other alterations are proposed, permission should be established before proceeding.
For landlords, we can also discuss furnishing and finish selections around the intended occupants, maintenance needs and agreed budget.
How Do You Make a Small Dalston Café Feel Welcoming?
Plan the entrance and service route before deciding how many seats to include.
Customers need to understand where to order, where to wait and how to reach a table. Staff need enough room to prepare, serve and clear without repeatedly crossing those routes.
We test counter positions and seating arrangements against the equipment and operating brief. Storage, cleaning and deliveries also need a place in the plan.
Colours, furniture and lighting then develop the atmosphere around a workable arrangement.

Can You Keep Character Without Making the Interior Feel Busy?
A personal interior benefits from a clear hierarchy.
You might choose one strong wall colour, a distinctive piece of furniture or a collection of artwork as the starting point. Quieter surrounding finishes give those elements room to be appreciated.
We consider the scale and repetition of colour, pattern and texture across the space. Concealed storage can also help, allowing everyday belongings to be put away while keeping selected objects on display.
This approach works for homes and businesses: character comes from considered choices rather than the number of features added.
