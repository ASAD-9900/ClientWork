How Can You Introduce Modern Interiors Into an Older Greenwich Home?
Begin with the features and proportions that give the property its character.
Contemporary furniture does not need to compete with an older interior. A simpler sofa, carefully positioned lighting or a restrained cabinet can sit comfortably alongside existing mouldings and fireplaces.
We consider what should remain visually prominent and where new elements can provide contrast. Storage, cabling and practical requirements are incorporated into the scheme rather than left until the end.
Where alterations may affect protected features, the relevant restrictions and specialist advice need to be established before implementation.
What Should You Plan Before Furnishing a Greenwich Peninsula Apartment?
Test the layout against the glazing, access routes and furniture you intend to use.
A large window can limit wall space for a television or storage unit. Reflections may affect a desk position, while an open kitchen can influence where the dining area works best.
We assess these relationships before selecting products. Window treatments, rugs and upholstered furniture can then help establish privacy, comfort and distinct areas within an open room.
Before ordering larger pieces, delivery routes and building access arrangements should also be checked.

Should You Involve an Interior Designer Before Extending?
Early interior planning helps you assess what the additional space needs to accommodate.
A proposed extension should be tested with actual furniture and kitchen dimensions. This can reveal whether there is enough room around dining chairs, where useful storage could sit and how the existing rooms connect to the new area.
We can develop the interior brief alongside your architect’s proposals, helping coordinate furniture, lighting and fitted elements while key decisions are still being considered.
The scope establishes who prepares each drawing and who is responsible for specialist information.
Can a Greenwich Café Serve Both Takeaway and Seated Customers?
A café needs clear places to order, wait, collect and sit down.
We explore the customer route alongside the equipment and service brief. Customers waiting for drinks should have somewhere to stand without blocking the entrance or interrupting seated guests.
Behind the counter, preparation, washing, refrigeration and storage need to be considered with the equipment supplier and operating team.
The atmosphere then develops through furniture, lighting and materials around a workable arrangement.
