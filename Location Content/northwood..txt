How Can You Make a Larger Northwood Home Feel More Connected?
Give each room a clear purpose, then consider the relationships between them.
A family kitchen, formal reception room and quieter sitting area do not need identical schemes. Related colours, timber finishes or selected metal details can help them feel comfortable alongside one another.
We also review furniture scale, doorways and movement through the house. A consistent design direction should support the different ways your household uses each space.
Discuss Your Whole-Home Design
Do You Need a Separate Preparation Kitchen or Walk-In Pantry?
The answer depends on your cooking habits and available space.
A separate preparation area may be useful for frequent entertaining or particular cooking routines. A pantry may improve food and appliance storage without requiring a second kitchen.
We discuss what you prepare, the equipment you use and how many people cook at the same time. The proposed arrangement should improve access and organisation while leaving sufficient space for the main kitchen and dining area.
How Can You Make a Generous Entrance Hall Feel Welcoming?
Balance furniture, lighting and storage with the hall’s proportions.
An entrance needs to accommodate everyday arrivals as well as establish the home’s character. A console, bench or occasional chair can be useful where there is sufficient space.
We consider sightlines, staircase access, coat storage and lighting at different heights. The arrangement should feel comfortable on arrival and remain easy to move through.
What Should You Decide Before Adding a Cinema or Leisure Room?
Establish how often it will be used and what experience you want.
An occasional film room may work with flexible seating and a television. A dedicated cinema involves different requirements for equipment, lighting, sound and ventilation.
We assess the room’s proportions, storage and relationship with adjoining spaces. Specialist audiovisual and acoustic input should inform the proposal where required.
The same approach applies to games rooms, gyms and other leisure spaces: the design should begin with how you intend to use them.
