Do I Need Permission to Refurbish a Barbican Flat?
Check both landlord approval and listed building consent before committing to alterations. These are separate processes, and the requirements depend on the proposed work.
The Barbican Estate’s buildings are Grade II listed. Its protection is not limited to external façades: internal features and layouts can also contribute to the building’s special interest.
The City of London’s guidance should be consulted before planning changes to partitions, original fittings or other building fabric. Some works may require listed building consent, while other proposals may not. Building regulations requirements must also be considered separately.
A useful first step is to gather:
Your existing floor plan and photographs.
A description of the changes you want to make.
Information about original features and previous alterations.
Any existing approvals or relevant property documents.
Oraanj can include design drawings and application support within the agreed scope, with specialist advice arranged where required.

Can I Improve My Barbican Kitchen Without Changing the Whole Layout?
Often, the most useful improvements begin within the existing footprint. Cabinet organisation, appliance placement, worktop space and lighting should be assessed before assuming walls or services need to move.
We consider how you cook, what you need to store and whether the kitchen should remain separate or connect more closely with adjoining spaces. Where original fittings survive, their significance and the applicable guidance need to inform the proposal.
Moving a kitchen or adding an island requires more than a furniture layout. Drainage, power, ventilation, circulation and any necessary permissions must be assessed before the design is confirmed.
For some homes, retaining the existing arrangement and improving its functionality will be the most appropriate solution.
What Should I Consider Before Changing Flooring or Adding Fitted Storage?
Flooring and joinery need to be planned around the building’s services as well as its appearance.
The Barbican Estate’s background heating uses electrical cables within the floors. The City of London advises residents not to make fixings that penetrate the floors because they could damage these cables. Flooring and underlay also affect heat transfer.
Before specifying a new floor, establish its compatibility with the heating system and the applicable estate requirements, including acoustic considerations. An underlay’s advertised sound-reduction figure alone does not establish that a complete floor construction is suitable.
For fitted storage, we consider fixing methods, access to services, ventilation and the treatment of original features. Freestanding furniture may be appropriate in some spaces, while fitted elements need properly assessed installation details.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I have to keep every original feature in my Barbican flat?
The appropriate approach depends on the feature, its significance and the proposed work. Do not assume that an internal fitting is unprotected simply because it is inside your home.
Review the estate’s listed building guidance and obtain advice before removing or altering original elements. We develop the design around what can appropriately be retained, repaired or changed.
Can you make a Barbican apartment feel brighter?
Yes. Furniture positioning, wall colours, window treatments and layered lighting can improve how light is experienced without changing the building’s windows or structure.
We assess the apartment’s orientation and layout, keeping useful daylight paths clear and adding task lighting where it is needed.
How much will a Barbican refurbishment cost?
Costs depend on the size and condition of the property, retained features, technical requirements and extent of the work.
Your budget should separate design fees, necessary surveys or specialist advice, approval-related costs, building works, joinery, furniture, installation, VAT and contingency.
We review the property and intended scope before preparing a proposal. A kitchen update, a furniture scheme and a complete refurbishment should be priced individually.
How long will the project take?
The programme needs to account for design development, any required consents, procurement and construction. Approval periods and bespoke manufacturing can affect the start date.
We establish these dependencies before confirming the works programme. Tell us your preferred completion date early so we can assess the scope and sequence realistically.
Can you work with my existing builder?
Yes. Our design-only service can provide the agreed drawings and specifications for your contractor. We establish who is responsible for surveys, specialist assessments, approvals and implementation.
Can I remain in the flat during refurbishment?
For furniture installation or limited decoration, this may be practical. Kitchen replacement, flooring works or extensive electrical alterations can make temporary accommodation more suitable.
We discuss access, dust, noise and the availability of essential facilities before work begins.
Can you design around my existing furniture and art?
Yes. Pieces you want to keep can form the starting point for the scheme. We assess their dimensions, placement and lighting, then develop complementary storage, finishes and furniture around them.
