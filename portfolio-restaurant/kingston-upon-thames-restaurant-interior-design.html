A Welcoming Restaurant with Different Ways to Dine

This Kingston upon Thames restaurant scheme brings together a main dining area, a statement bar and a more intimate setting for group bookings.

Terracotta banquettes give the dining room a warm identity, complemented by timber tables, muted green details and metallic accents. Illuminated back-bar shelving provides a focal point, while coordinated finishes connect the different guest areas.

The design approach balances atmosphere with practical operation: a clear arrival, comfortable seating, accessible service routes and storage positioned around daily requirements.

Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions. Detailed architectural coordination was resolved prior to site commencement using precise AutoCAD drawings and elevations.

01 Challenge

Organising Different Dining Settings

A venue with bar seating, regular tables and a private-group area needs clear relationships between its spaces.

Design Response

Develop the seating plan alongside guest circulation and staff routes. Confirm capacity from the measured layout and relevant project assessments rather than setting an unsupported target.

02 Challenge

Maintaining Comfortable Service Routes

Closely spaced tables can make serving, clearing and moving through the restaurant difficult.

Design Response

Check table dimensions, chair movement and access to banquettes together. Position service stations where they support the layout without becoming obstacles.

03 Challenge

Creating a More Private Group Setting

Visual separation does not automatically provide acoustic privacy.

Design Response

Establish whether the operator needs a visually distinct area or an enclosed room. Select screens or partitions against that requirement, with specialist acoustic input where necessary.

04 Challenge

Coordinating the Bar with Daily Operation

An illuminated display creates visual interest, but the working counter also needs practical equipment and storage arrangements.

Design Response

Establish the drinks menu and equipment schedule before detailing the joinery. Coordinate refrigeration, glasswashing, ice, waste, power and maintenance access.

05 Challenge

Selecting Entrance and Dining-Room Flooring

Entrance areas may experience moisture and dirt brought in from outside.

Design Response

Assess flooring for its location, expected use and cleaning regime. Consider entrance matting and maintenance alongside the product specification, avoiding claims that any finish will prevent all slips or stains.

06 Challenge

Adjusting the Atmosphere Throughout the Day

Different service periods require different lighting settings.

Design Response

Combine ambient, table, feature and task lighting with suitable controls. Test visibility at tables, circulation routes and service points during commissioning.

These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
