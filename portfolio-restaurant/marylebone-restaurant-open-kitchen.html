Kitchen Theatre with Comfortable Dining

This Marylebone restaurant scheme explores the relationship between cooking, service and the guest experience.

The approach considers more than appearance. Table arrangements, staff routes, equipment requirements and storage need to work together so the restaurant can operate comfortably throughout service.

Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions. Detailed architectural coordination was resolved prior to site commencement using precise electrical and lighting layout drawings.

01 Challenge

Integrating an Open Kitchen

Visible cooking introduces sound, heat and activity into the dining environment.

Design Response

Coordinate the kitchen interface and nearby seating with the kitchen designer and relevant building-services specialists. Cooking methods, equipment and ventilation requirements should inform the layout from the outset.

02 Challenge

If open-flame cooking is proposed, its feasibility and specialist requirements must be established before the interior is finalised.

Keeping Service Routes Practical Tables, counter seating and waiting positions can create congestion around the kitchen pass.

Design Response

Plan serving, clearing and replenishment routes alongside the furniture arrangement. Position service stations where they support staff without obstructing guest circulation.

03 Challenge

Balancing Intimate Seating with Flexibility

Fixed booths create distinct settings but need to accommodate comfortable access and practical cleaning.

Design Response

Develop banquette dimensions, table heights and surrounding clearances together. Combine fitted seating with movable tables and chairs where the layout supports it.

04 Challenge

Managing Sound in a Lively Dining Room

Conversation, equipment and tableware all contribute to the sound environment.

Design Response

Consider acoustic requirements alongside ceilings, wall finishes and furnishings. Upholstery may contribute to absorption, but booth partitions should not be described as providing acoustic privacy without supporting assessment.

05 Challenge

Selecting Materials for Daily Service

Dining surfaces and seating experience frequent spills, cleaning and wear.

Design Response

Confirm the exact materials and finishes against supplier information. Review cleaning, staining, abrasion and repair requirements rather than promising scratch-proof or stain-proof performance.

06 Challenge

Creating Atmosphere without Losing Visibility

Dining lighting needs to feel welcoming while supporting menus, movement and service.

Design Response

Coordinate ambient, table, feature and task lighting, with suitable controls. Test the settings during commissioning.

These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Marylebone Restaurant Fit-Out Cost?

Provisional interior fit-out allowance: £250,000–£550,000 excluding VAT.

This range applies to a defined scope involving interior design, finishes, fitted seating, joinery, furniture, decorative lighting and installation.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, drawings and specifications
  • Interior fit-out: Flooring, ceilings, partitions and decoration
  • Fitted seating and joinery: Banquettes, service stations and storage
  • Furniture: Tables, chairs and counter seating
  • Lighting and electrical work: Fittings, controls and agreed alterations
  • Specialist coordination: Interfaces with kitchen equipment and building services
  • Installation and handover: Delivery, fitting, commissioning coordination and snagging
  • Specialist cooking equipment, extraction, fire-suppression systems, refrigeration and major incoming-services upgrades require separate quotations unless expressly included.: Property acquisition, rent deposits, licensing costs, operating stock and pre-opening staffing are excluded. Professional fees and approval-related charges should be identified separately.

Allow a separate contingency of 10–15%, reviewed following survey, with VAT added where applicable.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

6–10 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 6–10 months, with several stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and feasibility: 2–4 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 4–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and specialist coordination: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals and landlord review: Confirm for the premises and proposed work
  • Procurement and manufacture: 8–14 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 10–16 weeks
  • Commissioning, snagging and handover: 2–3 weeks
  • Extraction feasibility, power availability, specialist equipment deliveries and approval requirements may extend the programme.: The opening date should also account for commissioning and operational preparations.
