Notting Hill Hospitality Design

A Mediterranean dining room organised around hearth cooking and conviviality

This 90-cover restaurant in Notting Hill was planned around an open wood-fired oven and charcoal grill. The space is divided by sculpted plaster archways into three interconnected dining salons, giving the restaurant a sense of intimacy and discovery rather than feeling like a single cavernous hall.

We selected tactile, honest materials that respond well to heat and age with character: hand-trowelled lime plaster, terracotta floor tiles, cast terrazzo counters, and solid oiled walnut dining tables. Curved booth seating along the perimeter allows parties of four to six to dine comfortably, while the counter surrounding the open hearth offers solo diners and couples a front-row view of the chefs at work.

Design Narrative & Detailing

Architectural Arches, Kitchen Sightlines & Robust Finishes

Designed to immerse diners in the craft of hearth cooking while maintaining smooth service flow for front-of-house staff.

Open-flame cooking generates heat, smoke, and aromas that require careful environmental engineering. We integrated a high-volume extraction hood disguised behind hand-trowelled plaster mouldings, maintaining pristine air quality throughout the dining areas. Banquette seating is upholstered in soil-resistant textured wool with leather piping, paired with solid timber tables that can be quickly wiped down and reset between covers. Low-level ceramic wall sconces cast warm pools of light across the curved plaster walls, setting a relaxed, unhurried evening tone.
