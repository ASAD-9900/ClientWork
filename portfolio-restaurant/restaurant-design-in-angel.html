Hospitality Architecture & Restaurant Design

One Venue, Different Dining Experiences

This Angel restaurant and café bar scheme brings together a welcoming entrance, sociable counter seating and comfortable dining areas.

Olive upholstery gives the interior a consistent identity, complemented by timber joinery and textured detailing. Banquette seating creates a defined setting around the room’s perimeter, while individual tables and bar stools offer different ways to use the space.

The design approach considers how the venue operates throughout the day. Ordering, preparation, table service and cleaning need to work alongside an atmosphere that can shift from informal café visits to longer evening meals.

The Design Brief

The supplied brief centres on creating a venue that supports three service periods: morning coffee, lunchtime dining and evening meals.

  • Coordinate café, bar and restaurant functions.
  • Provide a choice of counter, table and banquette seating.
  • Keep staff and guest circulation clear.
  • Connect the dining area with the kitchen pass.
  • Develop adjustable lighting for different service periods.
  • Select furniture and finishes appropriate for commercial use.
Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions.

01 Challenge

Accommodating Different Service Patterns

Takeaway coffee customers and seated diners use a venue differently. Poorly positioned ordering and collection points can create congestion.

Design Response

Plan arrival, ordering, collection and table-service routes together. Confirm the operator’s service model before finalising the layout.

02 Challenge

Balancing Seating with Comfortable Movement

A large seating target can compromise circulation if table numbers are fixed before clearances are checked.

Design Response

Develop banquettes, tables and counter seating alongside staff routes and access requirements. Confirm capacity from the coordinated layout and relevant project assessments.

03 Challenge

Connecting the Kitchen Pass with the Dining Room

The pass needs to support serving and clearing without becoming a gathering point that blocks circulation.

Design Response

Coordinate the pass position, service stations and storage with the kitchen team. Check equipment dimensions and routes between preparation, serving and dining areas.

04 Challenge

Managing Sound in a Busy Interior

Conversation, equipment and tableware all contribute to the sound environment.

Design Response

Consider acoustic requirements alongside ceilings, wall finishes and furnishings. Any proposed ceiling treatment should be specified for the actual room, with performance supported by appropriate information rather than appearance alone.

05 Challenge

Moving from Daytime to Evening Use

A café atmosphere and an evening dining setting need different lighting arrangements.

Design Response

Combine ambient, table, feature and task lighting with suitable controls. Test visibility at tables, service points and circulation routes during commissioning.

06 Challenge

Selecting Practical Materials

Seating, flooring and joinery experience repeated cleaning and wear throughout all-day service.

Design Response

Review supplier information for the intended use, maintenance requirements and repair options. Flooring suitability should be assessed for the location and expected conditions.

These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Angel Fit-Out Cost?

£200,000–£450,000 excluding VAT.

This is an initial editorial estimate for a defined interior fit-out involving finishes, bar joinery, fitted seating, furniture, decorative lighting and installation.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, drawings and specifications
  • Interior fit-out: Flooring, ceilings, decoration and agreed partitions
  • Bar and service joinery: Counter, storage, shelving and equipment interfaces
  • Seating and furniture: Banquettes, chairs, stools and tables
  • Lighting and electrical work: Fittings, controls and agreed alterations
  • Specialist coordination: Interfaces with kitchen equipment and building services
  • Installation and handover: Delivery, fitting, commissioning coordination and snagging
  • Commercial kitchen equipment, extraction, specialist refrigeration, substantial acoustic isolation and major services upgrades require separate quotations unless expressly included.: Property acquisition, rent deposits, licensing costs, operating stock and pre-opening staffing are excluded. Professional fees and approval-related charges should be identified separately.

Allow a separate contingency of 10–15%, reviewed following survey, with VAT added where applicable.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

6–10 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 6–10 months, with some stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and feasibility: 2–4 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 4–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and specialist coordination: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals and landlord review: Confirm for the premises and proposed work
  • Procurement and manufacture: 8–14 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 10–16 weeks
  • Commissioning, snagging and handover: 2–3 weeks
