A Neighbourhood Restaurant with Warmth and Character

This Greenwich restaurant scheme brings together a coordinated palette and a practical approach to all-day hospitality.

Olive upholstery and terracotta seating introduce colour, while timber tables and fluted joinery provide warmth and texture. Pale stone surfaces create contrast at the counter, giving the service area a clear identity within the dining room.

The design considers the experience from arrival to seating and service. A defined reception point, varied seating and coordinated lighting help establish a welcoming atmosphere for different types of visit.

Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions.

01 Challenge

Balancing Counter Service with Seated Dining

Customers ordering drinks or collecting takeaway items can interrupt dining areas if their routes are not considered early.

Design Response

Plan the entrance, ordering point, collection position and routes to tables together. The final arrangement should reflect whether the operator provides counter service, table service or a combination.

02 Challenge

Creating Flexible Seating Arrangements

A restaurant needs to accommodate different parties without compromising circulation.

Design Response

Coordinate banquettes with movable tables and chairs. Check table combinations, chair movement and service access before confirming seating capacity.

03 Challenge

Making the Counter Practical to Operate

A distinctive counter must also support preparation, cleaning and replenishment.

Design Response

Establish the menu and equipment schedule before detailing the joinery. Coordinate refrigeration, glasswashing, waste, power, plumbing and maintenance access.

04 Challenge

Managing Sound around Hard Surfaces

Timber, stone and conversation can contribute to a lively dining environment.

Design Response

Consider acoustic requirements alongside the ceiling, wall finishes and furniture. Upholstery and drapery may contribute to absorption, but neither establishes acoustic privacy or guarantees a particular result.

05 Challenge

Coordinating Building Services with the Premises

Cooking methods and equipment choices affect ventilation, drainage and electrical requirements.

Design Response

Survey existing services and obtain specialist input before finalising the fit-out. Any proposed extraction route or alteration to the building should be assessed for the actual property.

06 Challenge

Creating an Evening Atmosphere

A setting that works for morning coffee may need adjustment for longer evening visits.

Design Response

Combine ambient, table and task lighting with appropriate controls. Test visibility at tables, circulation routes and service points during commissioning.

These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed systems require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Greenwich Fit-Out Cost?

Provisional interior fit-out allowance: £150,000–£350,000 excluding VAT.

This allowance applies to a defined scope involving finishes, counter joinery, fitted seating, furniture, decorative lighting and installation.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, drawings and specifications
  • Interior fit-out: Flooring, ceilings, decoration and agreed partitions
  • Counter and service joinery: Reception, beverage counter, shelving and storage
  • Seating and furniture: Banquettes, chairs, stools and tables
  • Lighting and electrical work: Fittings, controls and agreed alterations
  • Specialist services: Defined ventilation, plumbing and acoustic requirements
  • Installation and handover: Delivery, fitting, commissioning coordination and snagging
  • Commercial kitchen equipment, extraction systems, specialist refrigeration, substantial acoustic isolation and major services upgrades require separate quotations unless expressly included.: Property acquisition, rent deposits, licensing costs, operating stock and pre-opening staffing are excluded. Professional fees and approval-related charges should be identified separately.

Allow a separate contingency of 10–15%, reviewed following survey, with VAT added where applicable.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

5–9 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 5–9 months, with some stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and feasibility: 2–4 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 3–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and specialist coordination: 4–8 weeks
  • Approvals and landlord review: Confirm for the premises and proposed work
  • Procurement and manufacture: 8–14 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 8–14 weeks
