Before You Design a Restaurant, Get These Five Decisions Right

Restaurant design starts long before choosing colours, chairs or pendant lights.

These five decisions influence almost everything that follows in the planning and commercial success of your space:

01
Who Is Your Customer? A neighbourhood café, destination restaurant, cocktail bar and quick-service concept need very different environments. We look at who you want to attract, how long customers are likely to stay and the experience you want them to remember.
02
How Many Covers Do You Need? More tables don't automatically create a better restaurant. The right layout needs to balance seating capacity with circulation, comfort, service and the character of the venue.
03
How Will Staff Move? Routes between dining areas, service points, bars and back-of-house spaces need to be considered early. A restaurant can look excellent but become difficult to operate if these relationships haven't been properly planned.
04
What Should the Restaurant Feel Like? Relaxed? Energetic? Intimate? Refined? Fast-moving? Lighting, materials, acoustics, furniture spacing and visual character all contribute to that experience.
05
What Is the Project Budget? The design needs to respond to the commercial reality of the project. Understanding the intended budget early helps us prioritise where investment matters most and develop the design accordingly.

Investment Considerations

How Much Does Restaurant Interior Design Cost in London?

There isn't one meaningful price per room for a restaurant. A 600 sq ft neighbourhood café and a 4,000 sq ft restaurant with a bar, bespoke joinery and substantial fit-out requirements are completely different projects.

SIZE OF PREMISES Larger spaces generally require more design and potentially more physical work across dining and service areas.
CONDITION An existing restaurant may require less intervention than an empty shell or premises being converted from another commercial use.
FIT-OUT LEVEL Changes to floors, ceilings, partitions, lighting, washrooms and services can significantly affect the wider project budget.
BESPOKE JOINERY Bars, counters, banquettes and specialist furniture represent an important part of the restaurant investment and visual impact.
FURNITURE & FINISHES The quantity, specification, acoustic properties, and sourcing route of furniture and surfaces all influence cost.
SPECIALIST WORKS Commercial kitchens, extraction, acoustics, fire strategy and other technical requirements may require specialist professional input.

Once we understand the premises, concept and intended scope, we can establish the appropriate design service and next steps.

Before You Sign a Restaurant Lease

Already Looking at a Restaurant Unit?

Where appropriate to the service, reviewing the potential space before committing heavily to a design can help identify important questions early.

Critical layout questions to consider:

  • Does the floor area support your intended concept and covers target?
  • Is the relationship between front and back of house workable?
  • Where will customers enter, wait, and order?
  • Is there enough practical storage for daily operations and dry goods?
  • What existing services (gas, water, three-phase power, drainage) are present?
  • What alterations may be required to structural walls or floor slabs?
  • Are there landlord or building-management restrictions on operating hours or extraction?
  • Which specialist consultants might be required?

Oraanj can help you understand the interior-design implications of a proposed space. Legal, planning, structural, fire, mechanical and other specialist matters should be confirmed by the relevant qualified professionals.

Restaurant Design Checklist

Planning a New Restaurant?

Before design begins, gathering this information helps focus the project around the right priorities:

THE PROPERTY

  • Floor plan or estate-agent plan
  • Property address & postcode
  • Approximate floor area
  • Existing photographs / video
  • Landlord pack / lease constraints

THE BUSINESS

  • Restaurant concept & identity
  • Cuisine & service style
  • Target customer demographic
  • Intended number of covers
  • Opening hours & service rhythm

THE PROJECT

  • Target opening date
  • Approximate project budget
  • Design requirements & scope
  • Existing contractors / specialists
  • Kitchen & bar equipment list

THE BRAND

  • Logo & brand guidelines
  • Reference restaurants & moodboards
  • Preferred acoustic & lighting feel
  • Colours & materials you love
  • Finishes or styles you dislike

The clearer the brief at the beginning, the easier it is to focus the design around the right priorities.
