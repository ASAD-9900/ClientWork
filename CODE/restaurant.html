Before You Design a Restaurant, Get These Five Decisions Right

Foundational Decisions That Drive Trading Success

Restaurant design starts long before choosing colours, chairs or pendant lights. These five decisions influence almost everything that follows in the planning and commercial success of your space.

01 — Who Is Your Customer?

A neighbourhood café, destination restaurant, cocktail bar and quick-service concept need very different environments. We analyze who you want to attract, how long customers are likely to stay, and the experience you want them to remember.

02 — How Many Covers Do You Need?

More tables don't automatically create a better restaurant. The right layout balances seating capacity with customer circulation, table spacing, dining comfort, and staff service rhythm.

03 — How Will Staff Move?

Routes between dining areas, service points, bars and back-of-house spaces need to be planned early. A restaurant can look striking but become frustrating to operate if these workflow relationships are overlooked.

04 — What Should the Restaurant Feel Like?

Relaxed? Energetic? Intimate? Refined? Fast-moving? Layered lighting, tactile materials, acoustic absorption, furniture spacing and visual warmth all combine to define that atmosphere.

05 — What Is the Project Budget?

The design needs to respond to the commercial reality of the project. Understanding your intended capex early helps us prioritize where investment delivers the highest return.

How Much Does Restaurant Interior Design Cost in London?

Transparent Capex Planning & Cost Drivers

There isn't one flat price per room for a restaurant. A 600 sq ft neighbourhood café and a 4,000 sq ft brasserie with an open kitchen, cocktail bar, and bespoke banquettes have completely different capex realities:

SIZE OF PREMISES Larger floor plates require more design and physical work across dining, bar and service areas.
EXISTING CONDITION An existing fitted restaurant needs less intervention than an empty shell or commercial change of use.
FIT-OUT COMPLEXITY Modifications to floors, ceilings, acoustic partitions, washrooms and MEP significantly affect budget.
BESPOKE JOINERY Bars, counters, banquettes and architectural woodwork represent an important part of the investment.
SPECIALIST WORKS Commercial kitchens, extraction flues, acoustic dampening and fire strategies require specialist input.
