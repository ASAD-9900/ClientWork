What Are Technical Interior Design Drawings?

Moving From the Approved Design Into Measured 2D Detail

A 3D visual helps you understand what an interior could look like. A technical interior drawing communicates specific information about the design in a measured two-dimensional format.

01 Arrangements

Furniture & Wall Arrangements

Communicating where furniture is positioned, how interior walls are arranged, and key room dimensions.

02 Elevations

Wall Elevations & Joinery

Showing how wall elevations are composed and how fitted joinery relates to the surrounding room.

03 Coordination

Lighting & Electrical Intent

Showing where proposed lighting is positioned and how sockets and switches relate to furniture and joinery.

04 Materials

Selected Finish Information

Identifying selected floor finishes, wall finishes, paint colours, joinery finishes, and decorative materials.

When Do You Need Technical Drawings?

Connecting Approved Visual Concepts With On-Site Implementation

Once the visual direction has been agreed, a 3D image or mood board alone may not provide enough information to understand dimensions, positions, and relationships between elements.

1. Your Contractor Needs More Detailed Information

When renovating one or several rooms, detailed drawings help communicate the relationship between different parts of the approved interior to your builder.

2. You're Planning Bespoke Joinery

Wardrobes, media walls, shelving, cabinetry, and other fitted elements need to be developed in relation to the surrounding room proportions.

3. You're Planning Lighting & Electrical Positions

Sockets, switches, and lighting positions need to relate directly to furniture, kitchens, joinery, and the way the room will be used.

Professional Responsibilities

What Technical Interior Drawings Do Not Replace

Clear Distinction Between Interior Design Intent & Specialist Engineering

Oraanj's technical interior drawings communicate the interior design intent within the agreed scope. Different professionals remain responsible for specialist engineering and fabrication.

1. Structural, M&E & Building Regulations

Separate specialist input is required for structural engineering, mechanical & electrical (M&E) engineering, HVAC, fire strategy, drainage, and statutory Building Regulations approvals.

2. Specialist Joinery & Kitchen Shop Drawings

Appointed joiners, kitchen suppliers, and specialist fabricators remain responsible for verifying site dimensions and producing manufacturing/shop drawings before production.

3. Electrical Installation, Testing & Certification

Qualified electrical professionals remain responsible for circuit design, electrical loads, cable sizing, distribution boards, safety testing, certification, and final installation.
