The Planning Bridge

What Is Interior Architecture?

The Planning Between an Existing Property and the Finished Interior

Interior architecture focuses on how the inside of a property is organised, planned and developed.

It sits between the existing building and the finished interior. Depending on the project, this involves space planning, internal reconfiguration, furniture dimensions, circulation paths, lighting schemes, electrical layouts, kitchens, bathrooms, bespoke joinery, wall elevations, and complete technical drawings.

The aim is simple: resolve important spatial and design decisions before they become urgent, expensive questions during construction.

Exploring Spatial Options

Start With the Existing Floor Plan

Then Ask What Could Work Better

One of the most valuable parts of interior architecture is testing different ways of using the same floor area.

Imagine an existing ground floor with a separate kitchen, a dining room rarely used, a disconnected living room, limited storage, and awkward circulation. There isn't automatically one correct answer:

01
Option 01 - Open It Up Create a modern, connected kitchen, dining and living arrangement that maximises natural daylight and social entertaining.
02
Option 02 - Keep Some Separation Improve relationship between rooms while retaining quieter, acoustic or formal spaces with pocket doors or crittall screens.
03
Option 03 - Replan the Functions Relocate zones across the property to make superior use of garden access, morning light, and functional daily storage.

We test and compare each option against your lifestyle, structural feasibility (with specialist professional verification where required), and budget.

The Details Need to Work Together

A Few Centimetres Can Matter

Interior architecture resolves the critical spatial relationships that cause frustration when left to site guesswork.

01 / Lighting

Dining Table + Pendant

The ceiling light must be centred over where the dining table will actually sit, not in the geometric centre of the empty room.

02 / Power

Bedside Sockets + Headboard

Sockets must align perfectly with side-table heights and bed widths rather than hiding awkwardly behind the headboard.

03 / Plumbing

Vanity Basin + Plumbing

First-fix pipework and waste pipes must coordinate with drawer cutouts and wall-hung vanity units before tiling.

04 / Storage

Wardrobe Doors + Clearance

Hinged doors require verified swing clearances from bedside tables, pendant drops, and ceiling cornices.

Interior Architect or Architect?

It Depends on What You're Changing

An interior architecture project focuses primarily on the internal space and how it is planned.

A registered architect may be required where a project involves broader external architectural changes, planning applications, extensions or structural alterations. Structural changes require a structural engineer.

Oraanj's role is centred on the interior. Where a wider renovation requires specialist professional input, the relevant appointments can be identified and coordinated as appropriate to the project.
