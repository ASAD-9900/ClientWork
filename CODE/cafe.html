The Business Model Changes the Design

Start With How You Serve Coffee

Before deciding what the café should look like, we need to understand how it needs to work.

A neighbourhood coffee shop where customers stay for an hour needs a different layout from a busy commuter café serving hundreds of takeaway drinks. A bakery café displaying fresh products needs something different again.

We start with questions such as:

  • How do customers order? At the counter, table or digitally?
  • How much of your trade is takeaway? A busy takeaway business needs a clear route from entrance to order, collection and exit.
  • What are you serving? Coffee only? Pastries? Cakes? Hot food? Brunch? The menu can influence counter space, display, storage and specialist equipment requirements.
  • How many customers do you want to seat? We consider seating capacity alongside comfort, circulation and service.
  • How long do customers typically stay? This can influence seating types, table sizes, charging points and the overall atmosphere.
  • What happens during your busiest hour? The café needs to work when the queue is longest, not only when it is quiet.
The Café Counter Is the Heart of the Space

Design the Workflow Before the Counter

The counter is often where customers order, pay, collect drinks and interact with your team.

It can also contain or coordinate coffee equipment, displays, storage and service functions. That makes its layout one of the most important design decisions in the café. We consider:

Once the workflow is understood, we can develop the counter around the operation as well as the design.

Your Customer Mix Shapes the Floor Plan

Sit-In, Takeaway or Both?

Mostly Takeaway

Priorities may include:

  • Clear ordering route
  • Efficient queue management
  • Fast collection point
  • Strong counter visibility
  • Limited but useful seating
  • Easy customer exit

Mostly Sit-In

Priorities may include:

  • Comfortable seating
  • Different table sizes
  • Longer customer dwell time
  • Lighting and atmosphere
  • Accessible power where appropriate
  • Good circulation between tables

Takeaway + Sit-In

The challenge is preventing one customer journey from disrupting the other.

A good layout allows takeaway customers to order and collect efficiently while seated customers can enjoy the café without feeling surrounded by a queue.

An existing café can sometimes already have useful infrastructure, furniture or equipment.

Look Beyond the Cost of the Furniture

What Should You Budget for a Café Fit-Out?

There is no sensible single cost for designing and fitting out a café without understanding the premises. The budget can be affected by:

EXISTING CONDITION Taking over a functioning café can be very different from starting with a shell unit.
SIZE Floor area influences materials, furniture and the extent of physical works.
COUNTER A bespoke coffee counter can be a significant element of both the design and fit-out.
EQUIPMENT Coffee machines, refrigeration, food preparation and other specialist equipment may affect the wider design and services.
JOINERY Banquette seating, counters, displays and storage can increase the amount of bespoke work.
SERVICES The existing and proposed electrical, plumbing, ventilation and other requirements can affect the wider project.
FINISHES Flooring, lighting, wall finishes, furniture and specification level all influence cost.
PROFESSIONAL Depending on the property and works, other consultants, permissions or approvals may also need to be considered.

We recommend establishing the business requirements, premises and design scope before setting a meaningful project budget.

Real-World Operational Testing

A Café Design Should Work at 8:30am

A café can look fantastic when it is empty. The real commercial test comes during the morning rush.

Imagine: Twelve customers arrive within five minutes. Four want takeaway flat whites. Two are waiting for drinks. Someone is choosing pastries. A supplier delivery arrives. Guests are seeking tables. Staff are moving at speed between the grinder, espresso machine, till, and collection station.

Dedicated Takeaway Route

Clear line from door to payment without crossing seated dining areas.

Waiting Pocket

Comfortable standing zone for drinks collection that keeps the queue moving.

Barista Ergonomics

Two-step motion economy between espresso machine, milk fridge, sink & till.

Merchandising Sightlines

Pastry displays positioned naturally in the queue line for effortless add-on sales.

Can everyone move without getting in each other's way? That's why we consider the operational rhythm of the café alongside interior styling. The finished space should photograph beautifully — but more importantly, it must generate revenue smoothly when the queue reaches the door.
