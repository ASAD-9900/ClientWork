Taking Over an Existing Café?

An existing café can sometimes already have useful infrastructure, furniture or equipment.

Before recommending a complete strip-out, we look at what is worth retaining and what is preventing the space from working properly. That might mean changing the coffee counter, customer queue, seating layout, lighting, furniture, wall finishes, flooring, signage positions, display areas or bespoke storage.

A focused refurbishment can be very different from fitting out an empty commercial unit. The right approach depends on the condition of the premises and what your new café concept requires.

Before You Sign a Café Lease

If you're considering a commercial unit for a new café, the interior implications are worth thinking about before committing to a detailed design.

Useful questions include:

  • Is there enough room for the café concept?
  • Can the counter and customer queue work within the space?
  • How much realistic seating could the unit support?
  • Where are the existing services?
  • Is there enough storage?
  • How will deliveries work?
  • Does the premises already have useful café infrastructure?
  • What specialist advice may be required?

Oraanj can advise on the interior-design and space-planning considerations within our scope. Lease, planning, licensing, structural, fire, mechanical and other specialist matters should be confirmed with the appropriate professionals.

Real-World Operational Testing

A Café Design Should Work at 8:30am

A café can look fantastic when it is empty. The real commercial test comes during the morning rush.

Imagine: Twelve customers arrive within five minutes. Four want takeaway flat whites. Two are waiting for drinks. Someone is choosing pastries. A supplier delivery arrives. Guests are seeking tables. Staff are moving at speed between the grinder, espresso machine, till, and collection station.

Dedicated Takeaway Route

Clear line from door to payment without crossing seated dining areas.

Waiting Pocket

Comfortable standing zone for drinks collection that keeps the queue moving.

Barista Ergonomics

Two-step motion economy between espresso machine, milk fridge, sink & till.

Merchandising Sightlines

Pastry displays positioned naturally in the queue line for effortless add-on sales.

Can everyone move without getting in each other's way? That's why we consider the operational rhythm of the café alongside interior styling. The finished space should photograph beautifully — but more importantly, it must generate revenue smoothly when the queue reaches the door.
