Mayfair Hospitality Architecture

An opulent subterranean lounge uniting honed marble, rich burgundy velvet and backlit amber joinery

Commissioned in the heart of prime Mayfair, this boutique cocktail lounge and destination bar was conceived to offer an exclusive sanctuary reminiscent of London's finest private members' clubs. The design brief called for an intimate, atmospheric drinking environment capable of sustaining brisk evening service while maintaining exceptional visual sophistication.

The venue was delivered through our integrated approach to interior designer in Mayfair and specialized bar interior design, orchestrating flawless front-of-house circulation with luxury craftsmanship.

The space is anchored by a sculptural marble counter paired with bespoke burgundy velvet barstools, illuminated back-bar display shelving, and brass accents. Intimate velvet banquette booths, fluted acoustic dividers, and a grand marble fireplace create private pockets of luxury, culminating in an exclusive private dining salon beneath a statement crystal chandelier.

Following our acclaimed work on the Kensington lounge bar project, this Mayfair venue delivers the ultimate benchmark in London cocktail culture.

Hospitality Detailing & Craftsmanship

Cocktail Ergonomics, Acoustic Discipline & Low-Glare Ambience

Balancing intense commercial bar service efficiency with sensory warmth, sound attenuation, and VIP exclusivity.

In luxury Mayfair venues, operational precision is as crucial as aesthetic impact. We engineered the central bar with commercial stainless steel speed rails, rapid ice wells, and integrated glassware chilling stations to streamline server workflows. Custom acoustic partitions wrapped in deep burgundy velvet buffer ambient chatter, allowing guests to converse comfortably even during peak hours.

Illumination is calibrated across four preset architectural scenes, shifting smoothly from warm dusk tones to a seductive, low-level 2200K amber ambience accented by wireless brass table lamps at every banquette.
