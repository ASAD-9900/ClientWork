Hospitality Architecture & Cocktail Lounge

A Distinctive Setting for Cocktails and Conversation

This Mayfair cocktail lounge scheme brings together a dramatic bar setting and quieter areas for guests to settle into.

Burgundy seating, warm metallic details and stone surfaces establish a rich material palette. Illuminated back-bar shelving provides a focal point, while curved banquettes and individual tables create different settings within the lounge.

The design balances visual character with the practical demands of hospitality: comfortable seating, clear service routes, accessible storage and lighting suited to both guests and staff.

Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions. Detailed architectural coordination was resolved prior to site commencement using precise architectural lighting layout drawings.

01 Challenge

Balancing Atmosphere with Visibility

Low lighting can create intimacy, but guests and staff still need to navigate, read menus and use tables comfortably.

Design Response

Coordinate ambient, table, feature and task lighting. Give staff appropriate working light behind the bar without overwhelming the guest-facing atmosphere.

02 Challenge

Lighting levels and controls should be tested during commissioning rather than judged from decorative fittings alone.

Organising Bar Service An attractive counter can become difficult to operate if equipment and storage are positioned without reference to the drinks menu.

Design Response

Develop the under-bar arrangement around preparation, ice, refrigeration, glasswashing, waste and replenishment. Check equipment dimensions, staff movement and maintenance access before manufacture.

03 Challenge

The final arrangement should reflect the operator’s menu and service model.

Creating Intimate Seating without Congestion Booths and curved banquettes can define smaller settings, but their placement must preserve circulation.

Design Response

Coordinate table sizes, chair movement and service access together. Confirm seating capacity from the measured layout and relevant project assessments.

04 Challenge

Managing Sound in an Evening Venue

Music, conversation and bar activity affect both the guest experience and neighbouring spaces.

Design Response

Establish the intended operating hours and music use early. Obtain specialist acoustic advice where required, distinguishing sound absorption within the room from sound insulation between spaces.

05 Challenge

Upholstery and decorative dividers should not be presented as proof of sound isolation.

Specifying Finishes for Hospitality Use Stone counters, velvet seating and metallic details require appropriate specification and maintenance.

Design Response

Review samples and supplier information for the intended application. Consider spills, cleaning methods, abrasion and repairability before final selection.

06 Challenge

Coordinating Building Services

Ventilation, cooling, drainage and electrical requirements can materially affect the layout and fit-out.

Design Response

Survey existing provision and coordinate specialist designs before cabinetry, ceilings and finishes are finalised. Any basement-specific requirements must be established from the actual premises.

These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, installed systems and client revisions require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Mayfair Bar Fit-Out Cost?

£250,000–£600,000 excluding VAT.

This is a broad planning allowance for an agreed interior fit-out involving finishes, fitted seating, bar joinery, decorative lighting, furniture and installation. Its suitability depends on floor area, existing condition and specification.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, drawings and specifications
  • Interior fit-out: Flooring, ceilings, partitions and decoration
  • Bar and back-bar joinery: Counter, display shelving, storage and equipment interfaces
  • Seating and furniture: Banquettes, stools, tables and loose seating
  • Lighting and electrical work: Fittings, controls and agreed alterations
  • Specialist services: Defined ventilation, cooling, plumbing and acoustic requirements
  • Installation and handover: Delivery, fitting, commissioning coordination and snagging
  • Specialist bar equipment, refrigeration, glasswashing, audio systems, substantial acoustic isolation and major services upgrades require separate quotations unless expressly included.: The allowance excludes property acquisition, rent deposits, operating stock, licensing costs and pre-opening staffing. Professional fees and approval-related charges should be identified separately.

Allow a separate contingency of 10–15%, reviewed following survey, with VAT added where applicable.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

6–12 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 6–12 months, with several stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and feasibility: 3–5 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 4–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and specialist coordination: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals and landlord review: Confirm for the premises and proposed use
  • Procurement and manufacture: 8–16 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 12–20 weeks
  • Commissioning, snagging and handover: 2–3 weeks
