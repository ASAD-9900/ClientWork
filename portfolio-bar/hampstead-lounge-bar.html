Hospitality Architecture & Cocktail Lounge

A Relaxed Setting with Natural Character

This Hampstead lounge and bar scheme takes its direction from natural textures, planting and comfortable seating.

Timber finishes and woven details create a warm backdrop, while greenery introduces colour and variation. Textured upholstery and smaller seating arrangements give guests different places to settle, from sociable dining tables to quieter lounge corners.

The design approach considers how the venue can feel welcoming during the day and more intimate in the evening, without losing the practical visibility and circulation needed for service.

The Design Brief

  • Establish a consistent interior palette using timber, woven textures and greenery.
  • Provide a choice of dining and lounge seating.
  • Support movement between the entrance, tables and bar.
  • Create lighting suitable for daytime and evening service.
  • Integrate practical storage and service requirements.
  • Select finishes appropriate for hospitality use.
  • The atmosphere should support the venue’s offering while remaining practical for the team operating it.
Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions. Detailed architectural coordination was resolved prior to site commencement using precise 3D CGI architectural rendering.

01 Challenge

Supporting Both Daytime and Evening Use

A venue that serves throughout the day needs more than one lighting setting.

Design Response

Combine decorative pendants with ambient and task lighting, using suitable controls to adjust the atmosphere. Coordinate this with available daylight and test table visibility during commissioning.

02 Challenge

Explore our lighting design services.

Introducing Greenery without Obstructing the Space Planting can become difficult to maintain or interfere with circulation when considered only as decoration.

Design Response

Plan planting positions alongside seating, lighting and access routes. Establish whether plants are live or artificial, how they will be maintained, and how any suspended elements will be supported.

03 Challenge

Greenery should not be presented as a substitute for assessed acoustic treatment.

Balancing Intimate Seating with Clear Service Routes Smaller seating pockets can create character, but their arrangement must allow staff and guests to move comfortably.

Design Response

Coordinate table sizes, chair movement, banquette positions and service access. Confirm capacity from the measured layout and relevant project assessments.

04 Challenge

Making the Bar Practical to Operate

A visually prominent bar also needs an efficient arrangement behind the counter.

Design Response

Develop the layout around the drinks menu, staffing model and equipment schedule. Coordinate refrigeration, glasswashing, ice, waste and storage before manufacture.

05 Challenge

The bar’s shape should follow the premises and operational needs rather than be assumed from the concept.

Specifying Textured Materials for Commercial Use Woven finishes and textured fabrics contribute to the atmosphere but need careful selection.

Design Response

Review supplier information for cleaning, durability and suitability for the intended application. Upholstery performance should be confirmed for the complete specified construction.

06 Challenge

Managing Sound and Equipment Noise

Conversation, music, glasswashing and refrigeration all contribute to the sound environment.

Design Response

Coordinate equipment placement and obtain specialist acoustic advice where required. Avoid promises of eliminating noise or achieving a particular acoustic result without supporting assessment.

These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, installed systems and client-requested changes require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Hampstead Lounge Fit-Out Cost?

£150,000–£350,000 excluding VAT.

This allowance is intended for a defined interior fit-out involving finishes, bar joinery, fitted seating, furniture, decorative lighting, planting features and installation.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, drawings and specifications
  • Interior finishes: Flooring, decoration, ceilings and agreed partitions
  • Bar and service joinery: Counter, shelving, storage and equipment interfaces
  • Seating and furniture: Banquettes, lounge seating, tables and chairs
  • Lighting and electrical work: Fittings, controls and agreed alterations
  • Planting features: Containers, support requirements and maintenance access
  • Installation and handover: Delivery, fitting, snagging and agreed documentation
  • Specialist bar equipment, commercial kitchen equipment, substantial acoustic isolation and major ventilation or services upgrades require separate quotations unless expressly included.: Property acquisition, rent deposits, licensing costs, operating stock and pre-opening staffing are excluded. Professional fees and approval-related charges should be identified separately.

Allow a separate contingency of 10–15%, reviewed following survey, with VAT added where applicable.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

5–9 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 5–9 months, with some stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and feasibility: 2–4 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 4–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and specialist coordination: 4–8 weeks
  • Approvals and landlord review: Confirm for the premises and proposed work
  • Procurement and manufacture: 8–14 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 8–14 weeks
  • Commissioning, snagging and handover: 1–3 weeks
