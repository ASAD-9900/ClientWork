Hospitality Architecture & Cocktail Lounge

Classic Character for a Contemporary West End Venue

This Covent Garden bar and lounge scheme takes inspiration from traditional London club interiors, pairing architectural detailing with a contemporary approach to hospitality.

A statement stone counter establishes the bar as a focal point. Timber joinery, metallic accents and upholstered seating introduce warmth, while decorative lighting gives individual tables and lounge settings their own identity.

The design considers the relationship between arrival, ordering, seating and service. Different settings allow the venue to accommodate shorter visits alongside more leisurely evenings.

Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions. Detailed architectural coordination was resolved prior to site commencement using precise lighting design drawings.

01 Challenge

Accommodating Busy Arrival Periods

A theatre-district venue may experience concentrated arrivals and departures, placing pressure on entrances, ordering points and circulation.

Design Response

Plan the entrance, waiting positions, bar access and table-service routes together. Assess the layout against the operator’s expected service pattern before confirming capacity.

02 Challenge

Balancing Atmosphere with Efficient Service

An intimate interior still needs clear routes for serving, clearing and replenishment.

Design Response

Position service stations and storage around the proposed seating arrangement. Check chair movement, tray access and routes between the bar and tables during layout development.

03 Challenge

Coordinating the Bar with the Drinks Menu

The counter’s appearance is only one part of its function. Equipment and storage arrangements influence daily operation.

Design Response

Establish the drinks offer, staffing model and equipment schedule before detailing the bar. Coordinate preparation areas, ice, refrigeration, glasswashing, waste and maintenance access.

04 Challenge

Managing Sound within the Lounge

Stone surfaces, conversation and music can combine to make a venue uncomfortable.

Design Response

Consider acoustic requirements alongside ceilings, wall finishes and furnishings. Upholstery can contribute to sound absorption, but room performance and sound transfer to neighbouring spaces require separate assessment where relevant.

05 Challenge

Selecting Finishes for Spills and Frequent Cleaning

Bar surfaces and seating experience repeated contact with drinks, cleaning products and furniture movement.

Design Response

Review supplier information for the intended application and maintenance regime. Confirm flooring suitability through appropriate assessment rather than relying on a single product rating or describing materials as stain-proof.

06 Challenge

Providing Different Lighting Settings

Early-evening service and late-night use may require different atmospheres.

Design Response

Coordinate ambient, table, feature and task lighting with suitable controls. Test visibility at tables and service points during commissioning.

These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Covent Garden Fit-Out Cost?

£200,000–£450,000 excluding VAT.

This allowance applies to a defined interior fit-out involving finishes, bar joinery, fitted seating, furniture, decorative lighting and installation.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, drawings and specifications
  • Interior fit-out: Flooring, ceilings, partitions and decoration
  • Bar and service joinery: Counter, displays, storage and equipment interfaces
  • Seating and furniture: Booths, chairs, stools and tables
  • Lighting and electrical work: Fittings, controls and agreed alterations
  • Specialist services: Defined ventilation, plumbing and acoustic requirements
  • Installation and handover: Delivery, fitting, commissioning coordination and snagging
  • Specialist bar equipment, refrigeration, glasswashing, commercial kitchen equipment, substantial acoustic isolation and major services upgrades require separate quotations unless expressly included.: Property acquisition, rent deposits, operating stock, licensing costs and pre-opening staffing are excluded. Professional fees and approval-related charges should be identified separately.

Allow a separate contingency of 10–15%, reviewed following survey, with VAT added where applicable.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

6–10 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 6–10 months, with several stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and feasibility: 2–4 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 4–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and specialist coordination: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals and landlord review: Confirm for the premises and proposed work
  • Procurement and manufacture: 8–14 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 10–16 weeks
  • Commissioning, snagging and handover: 2–3 weeks
