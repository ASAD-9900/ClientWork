One Venue, Different Dining Experiences
This Angel restaurant and café bar scheme brings together a welcoming entrance, sociable counter seating and comfortable dining areas.
Olive upholstery gives the interior a consistent identity, complemented by timber joinery and textured detailing. Banquette seating creates a defined setting around the room’s perimeter, while individual tables and bar stools offer different ways to use the space.
The design approach considers how the venue operates throughout the day. Ordering, preparation, table service and cleaning need to work alongside an atmosphere that can shift from informal café visits to longer evening meals.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating a venue that supports three service periods: morning coffee, lunchtime dining and evening meals.
The principal objectives are to:
Coordinate café, bar and restaurant functions.
Provide a choice of counter, table and banquette seating.
Keep staff and guest circulation clear.
Connect the dining area with the kitchen pass.
Develop adjustable lighting for different service periods.
Select furniture and finishes appropriate for commercial use.
Design Challenges and Responses
Accommodating Different Service Patterns
Takeaway coffee customers and seated diners use a venue differently. Poorly positioned ordering and collection points can create congestion.
Design response: Plan arrival, ordering, collection and table-service routes together. Confirm the operator’s service model before finalising the layout.
Balancing Seating with Comfortable Movement
A large seating target can compromise circulation if table numbers are fixed before clearances are checked.
Design response: Develop banquettes, tables and counter seating alongside staff routes and access requirements. Confirm capacity from the coordinated layout and relevant project assessments.
Connecting the Kitchen Pass with the Dining Room
The pass needs to support serving and clearing without becoming a gathering point that blocks circulation.
Design response: Coordinate the pass position, service stations and storage with the kitchen team. Check equipment dimensions and routes between preparation, serving and dining areas.
Managing Sound in a Busy Interior
Conversation, equipment and tableware all contribute to the sound environment.
Design response: Consider acoustic requirements alongside ceilings, wall finishes and furnishings. Any proposed ceiling treatment should be specified for the actual room, with performance supported by appropriate information rather than appearance alone.
Moving from Daytime to Evening Use
A café atmosphere and an evening dining setting need different lighting arrangements.
Design response: Combine ambient, table, feature and task lighting with suitable controls. Test visibility at tables, service points and circulation routes during commissioning.
Selecting Practical Materials
Seating, flooring and joinery experience repeated cleaning and wear throughout all-day service.
Design response: Review supplier information for the intended use, maintenance requirements and repair options. Flooring suitability should be assessed for the location and expected conditions.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
Olive Banquette Seating
Upholstered seating establishes a warm backdrop, with dimensions developed around comfort, table positions and practical cleaning.
A Sociable Café Bar
Counter seating offers an alternative to conventional tables, with the working side coordinated around equipment, storage and staff movement.
Warm Timber Joinery
Repeated timber tones connect the entrance, bar and seating areas through one consistent material direction.
A Kitchen-Facing Setting
Where the operational layout supports it, seating near the kitchen can make preparation and service part of the guest experience.
Layered Lighting
Decorative fittings provide character, supported by practical lighting for food service, circulation and cleaning.

The menu, service model, equipment schedule and seating layout should be coordinated before manufacture begins.
Later changes should be reviewed for their cost, programme and technical implications before proceeding. This helps the operator make informed decisions and maintain control of the agreed scope.
