A Welcoming Restaurant with Different Ways to Dine
This Kingston upon Thames restaurant scheme brings together a main dining area, a statement bar and a more intimate setting for group bookings.
Terracotta banquettes give the dining room a warm identity, complemented by timber tables, muted green details and metallic accents. Illuminated back-bar shelving provides a focal point, while coordinated finishes connect the different guest areas.
The design approach balances atmosphere with practical operation: a clear arrival, comfortable seating, accessible service routes and storage positioned around daily requirements.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating a flexible hospitality venue that accommodates relaxed dining, drinks and group gatherings.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a welcoming entrance and clear reception point.
Connect the dining room and bar without obstructing circulation.
Provide a mix of banquette, table and counter seating.
Create a distinct setting for private-group bookings.
Coordinate lighting for different service periods.
Select finishes appropriate for frequent commercial use.
Design Challenges and Responses
Organising Different Dining Settings
A venue with bar seating, regular tables and a private-group area needs clear relationships between its spaces.
Design response: Develop the seating plan alongside guest circulation and staff routes. Confirm capacity from the measured layout and relevant project assessments rather than setting an unsupported target.
Maintaining Comfortable Service Routes
Closely spaced tables can make serving, clearing and moving through the restaurant difficult.
Design response: Check table dimensions, chair movement and access to banquettes together. Position service stations where they support the layout without becoming obstacles.
Creating a More Private Group Setting
Visual separation does not automatically provide acoustic privacy.
Design response: Establish whether the operator needs a visually distinct area or an enclosed room. Select screens or partitions against that requirement, with specialist acoustic input where necessary.
Coordinating the Bar with Daily Operation
An illuminated display creates visual interest, but the working counter also needs practical equipment and storage arrangements.
Design response: Establish the drinks menu and equipment schedule before detailing the joinery. Coordinate refrigeration, glasswashing, ice, waste, power and maintenance access.
Selecting Entrance and Dining-Room Flooring
Entrance areas may experience moisture and dirt brought in from outside.
Design response: Assess flooring for its location, expected use and cleaning regime. Consider entrance matting and maintenance alongside the product specification, avoiding claims that any finish will prevent all slips or stains.
Adjusting the Atmosphere Throughout the Day
Different service periods require different lighting settings.
Design response: Combine ambient, table, feature and task lighting with suitable controls. Test visibility at tables, circulation routes and service points during commissioning.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
Illuminated Back-Bar Shelving
Integrated lighting gives the bar a clear presence within the restaurant, with display arrangements coordinated around storage and cleaning access.
Terracotta Banquette Seating
Warm upholstery establishes a consistent backdrop for the dining area. Seat dimensions and materials should be selected for their intended commercial use.
A Defined Arrival Point
A host station helps guests understand where to wait and allows the operator to manage seating without disrupting the entrance.
A Private-Group Setting
A distinct dining arrangement offers another booking option, with its size and level of separation established during detailed planning.
Coordinated Timber and Metallic Details
Repeated finishes connect furniture, counter joinery and decorative elements without making every area identical.

The menu, equipment requirements, seating layout and private-booking arrangements should be coordinated before manufacture begins.
Later changes should be reviewed for their cost, programme and technical implications before proceeding. This gives the operator a clear basis for decisions and helps maintain control of the agreed scope.
