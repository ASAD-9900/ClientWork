A Distinctive Setting for Cocktails and Conversation
This Mayfair cocktail lounge scheme brings together a dramatic bar setting and quieter areas for guests to settle into.
Burgundy seating, warm metallic details and stone surfaces establish a rich material palette. Illuminated back-bar shelving provides a focal point, while curved banquettes and individual tables create different settings within the lounge.
The design balances visual character with the practical demands of hospitality: comfortable seating, clear service routes, accessible storage and lighting suited to both guests and staff.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating an intimate cocktail venue with a strong identity and a practical service layout.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish the bar as the visual centrepiece.
Provide a choice of counter, lounge and booth seating.
Support efficient drink preparation and table service.
Create a more private setting for smaller gatherings.
Coordinate lighting, materials and furniture throughout.
Consider cleaning, maintenance and commercial durability.
The design approach gives equal attention to what guests experience and how the team operates behind the scenes.
Design Challenges and Responses
Balancing Atmosphere with Visibility
Low lighting can create intimacy, but guests and staff still need to navigate, read menus and use tables comfortably.
Design response: Coordinate ambient, table, feature and task lighting. Give staff appropriate working light behind the bar without overwhelming the guest-facing atmosphere.
Lighting levels and controls should be tested during commissioning rather than judged from decorative fittings alone.
Organising Bar Service
An attractive counter can become difficult to operate if equipment and storage are positioned without reference to the drinks menu.
Design response: Develop the under-bar arrangement around preparation, ice, refrigeration, glasswashing, waste and replenishment. Check equipment dimensions, staff movement and maintenance access before manufacture.
The final arrangement should reflect the operator’s menu and service model.
Creating Intimate Seating without Congestion
Booths and curved banquettes can define smaller settings, but their placement must preserve circulation.
Design response: Coordinate table sizes, chair movement and service access together. Confirm seating capacity from the measured layout and relevant project assessments.
Managing Sound in an Evening Venue
Music, conversation and bar activity affect both the guest experience and neighbouring spaces.
Design response: Establish the intended operating hours and music use early. Obtain specialist acoustic advice where required, distinguishing sound absorption within the room from sound insulation between spaces.
Upholstery and decorative dividers should not be presented as proof of sound isolation.
Specifying Finishes for Hospitality Use
Stone counters, velvet seating and metallic details require appropriate specification and maintenance.
Design response: Review samples and supplier information for the intended application. Consider spills, cleaning methods, abrasion and repairability before final selection.
Coordinating Building Services
Ventilation, cooling, drainage and electrical requirements can materially affect the layout and fit-out.
Design response: Survey existing provision and coordinate specialist designs before cabinetry, ceilings and finishes are finalised. Any basement-specific requirements must be established from the actual premises.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, installed systems and client revisions require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
A Statement Bar
A stone-finished counter and illuminated back-bar display create a clear focal point. The detailed design should resolve equipment integration, storage, cleaning and access.
Burgundy Lounge Seating
Curved banquettes and upholstered stools introduce warmth and continuity, with seating dimensions and fabrics selected for their intended commercial use.
Smaller Social Settings
Booths and individual tables offer different levels of intimacy while allowing the operator to accommodate varying group sizes.
A Private Dining Setting
A separate dining arrangement provides another guest experience within the venue, supported by coordinated furniture and feature lighting.
Layered Evening Lighting
Back-bar illumination, table lighting and decorative fittings create depth, with separate controls supporting service, cleaning and different operating periods.
