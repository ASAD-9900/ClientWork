The Salon Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on making effective use of a long commercial unit while maintaining comfortable working and circulation space.
The principal objectives are to:
Create a recognisable reception and checkout point.
Coordinate styling chairs, mirrors and storage.
Keep tools, cables and products organised.
Provide a comfortable wash area.
Integrate retail display without obstructing movement.
Select finishes appropriate for salon cleaning and daily use.
The number of styling positions should follow the measured layout, equipment dimensions and staffing requirements.
Design Challenges and Responses
Balancing Styling Capacity with Working Space
Adding stations can reduce the space available for stylists to move around clients.
Design response: Plan chair positions, mirror units and circulation together. Check working clearances with chairs in use before confirming capacity.
Coordinating Lighting for Hair Services
Decorative mirror lighting alone may not provide suitable illumination for cutting and colour assessment.
Design response: Coordinate mirror lighting with overhead task lighting, considering colour rendering, consistency, glare and shadows. Confirm performance through product information and on-site review.
Explore our lighting design services.
Keeping Tools and Cables Organised
Hairdryers, styling tools and charging cables can clutter work surfaces and circulation routes.
Design response: Integrate suitable tool storage and cable management into the joinery. Coordinate electrical connections and heat protection with the selected equipment.
Providing Reliable Wash-Area Services
Several basins operating together can place demands on water supply, heating and drainage.
Design response: Establish the expected usage and basin specifications before services are designed. Have appropriate specialists assess capacity rather than selecting a cylinder size from appearance or promising unlimited supply.
Selecting Surfaces for Colour Products and Cleaning
Hair colourants and cleaning products can affect worktops, flooring and cabinetry.
Design response: Review the actual materials against supplier guidance and the salon’s products. Confirm cleaning procedures and resistance to expected exposure; avoid describing finishes as universally stain-proof or chemical-proof.
Creating a More Relaxed Wash Setting
Running water, conversation and dryers can affect the atmosphere around the basins.
Design response: Consider the wash area’s position, lighting and acoustic requirements together. Any acoustic treatment must also be suitable for its location and maintenance needs.
These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
A Defined Reception
A coordinated desk and entrance setting give clients a clear place to check in and arrange their next appointment.
Arched Styling Mirrors
Repeated mirror shapes create a consistent visual rhythm, with lighting developed around both appearance and practical use.
Timber-Toned Storage
Fitted cabinetry can accommodate towels, tools and products, with internal arrangements based on the salon’s working routines.
Greenery and Warm Finishes
Planting introduces colour and softness, with placement and maintenance coordinated around the working floor.
A Dedicated Wash Area
Reclining basins establish a distinct part of the appointment journey, supported by appropriate services, access and lighting.
