A Coordinated Workplace with a Welcoming Arrival
This Croydon office scheme uses a consistent material palette to connect visitor-facing areas with private working spaces.
A fluted olive-green reception desk provides a distinctive arrival point. Its pale countertop contrasts with the textured cabinetry, while upholstered waiting furniture introduces a softer setting nearby.
Within the private office, coordinated shelving and cupboards provide a combination of display and concealed storage. Dark-framed glazed partitions define the circulation areas, maintaining visual connection between rooms.

The Workplace Brief
The design direction centres on creating a professional setting that feels welcoming to visitors and organised for everyday work.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a clearly defined reception.
Coordinate the private office with the visitor-facing areas.
Introduce useful storage without overwhelming the rooms.
Provide comfortable waiting furniture.
Maintain clear circulation between spaces.
Integrate practical lighting, power and equipment requirements.
For a comparable project, staffing, visitor numbers and storage needs would be established before finalising the layout.
Design Challenges and Responses
Creating a Reception with a Clear Identity
A reception desk needs to provide a focal point while supporting daily administrative tasks.
Design response: Use the fluted olive-green cabinetry to establish the design language, with the working side planned around equipment, storage and staff access.
Balancing Display with Practical Storage
Open shelves can give an office character, but documents and everyday supplies often need closed storage.
Design response: Combine selected display shelving with cupboards. Develop internal arrangements around the actual items being stored, including any security requirements.
Connecting Rooms without Making Them Identical
Reception, waiting and private working spaces serve different purposes.
Design response: Repeat the olive cabinetry, neutral tones and restrained detailing across the scheme, while varying furniture and storage to suit each room.
Providing Privacy within a Glazed Layout
Glass partitions define rooms but do not automatically provide visual or acoustic confidentiality.
Design response: Establish the privacy required for the office’s activities. Assess screening, doors and the complete partition construction where confidential conversations are expected.
Keeping Circulation Clear
Reception furniture and waiting seats can become obstacles if their dimensions are considered too late.
Design response: Coordinate desk positions, chair movement, door openings and routes between rooms. Consider different mobility requirements during layout development.
Coordinating Furniture with Building Services
Power points, cables and lighting need to support the furniture arrangement.
Design response: Confirm equipment requirements before installation. Coordinate connections with desks and joinery, retaining suitable access for maintenance.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme, rather than verified accounts of site problems or client revisions.
Key Design Features
A Fluted Olive-Green Reception Desk
The textured desk front gives the arrival area a recognisable identity, contrasted with a smooth, pale countertop.
Coordinated Office Cabinetry
Olive-green shelving and closed cupboards create a consistent backdrop to the private office.
A Contemporary Desk Setting
The desk and surrounding storage form a defined working area, with final dimensions and seating selected around user requirements.
Comfortable Waiting Furniture
An upholstered chair and stone-toned side table introduce a quieter setting for visitors.
Dark-Framed Glazed Partitions
Slim framing distinguishes rooms from the corridor and provides contrast against the cabinetry and lighter finishes.
