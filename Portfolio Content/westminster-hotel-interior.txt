A Welcoming Journey from Reception to Guest Room
This Westminster hotel scheme brings residential warmth to the guest experience through coordinated colours, furniture and architectural detailing.
A statement reception counter establishes the arrival point. Beyond it, lounge seating and dining arrangements offer places to pause, meet and spend time outside the bedroom.
Warm neutral tones, timber finishes and selected olive accents connect the public spaces with the guest accommodation. Decorative pendants introduce character, while integrated lighting gives corridors and bedrooms a consistent design direction.
The approach considers the hotel as a sequence of connected experiences rather than a collection of separate rooms.

The Hotel Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating a consistent hospitality identity from arrival through to the guest room.
The principal objectives are to:
Make reception easy to identify.
Provide comfortable lounge and dining settings.
Establish clear routes to guest accommodation.
Coordinate bedroom furniture, storage and lighting.
Select finishes appropriate for frequent cleaning and use.
Consider housekeeping and maintenance alongside guest comfort.
For a comparable project, room numbers, room types and the extent of public-area refurbishment would be established before developing the budget.
Design Challenges and Responses
Connecting Public Areas with Guest Rooms
Different parts of a hotel serve different purposes but should still feel related.
Design response: Establish a shared palette of timber tones, upholstery colours and selected details. Vary furniture and lighting to suit reception, dining, circulation and sleeping areas.
Making Arrival Clear and Welcoming
A visually striking lobby also needs to accommodate check-in, waiting and luggage.
Design response: Coordinate the reception counter, seating and circulation around the expected arrival pattern. Consider staff equipment and storage on the working side of the desk.
Balancing Bedroom Comfort with Practical Use
A guest room must accommodate sleeping, dressing, luggage and charging without feeling crowded.
Design response: Plan the bed, bedside furniture, wardrobe and luggage provision together. Check door swings and circulation before finalising fitted joinery.
Creating Suitable Bedroom Lighting
One lighting setting cannot comfortably serve reading, dressing and night-time movement.
Design response: Combine ambient, bedside and task lighting with straightforward controls. Review glare, switch positions and access from the bed during detailed design.
Specifying for Housekeeping and Maintenance
Decorative finishes need to withstand repeated cleaning and occasional repair.
Design response: Review upholstery, flooring, countertops and joinery against their intended use. Consider accessible fittings, replaceable components and the hotel’s maintenance procedures.
Coordinating Refurbishment with Hotel Operation
Work can affect guest access, housekeeping routes and available rooms.
Design response: Establish whether the hotel will close, operate in phases or take selected rooms out of service. Programme deliveries and disruptive activities against the agreed operational plan.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
A Sculptural Reception Counter
The reception provides a clear visual anchor, with its dimensions and working arrangements developed around staff and guest requirements.
Comfortable Lounge Settings
Grouped seating creates places for informal meetings, waiting and relaxation within the shared areas.
Coordinated Dining Spaces
Furniture and lighting distinguish the dining setting while maintaining continuity with the lounge.
Warm Corridor Detailing
Panelling and architectural lighting establish a consistent route towards the guest rooms. Acoustic performance requires separate assessment rather than being inferred from finishes.
Tailored Bedroom Joinery
Headboards, bedside furniture and wardrobes can be coordinated around the room layout, with integrated lighting and suitable maintenance access.

Managing Design Changes
Room layouts, furniture schedules, finish samples and lighting controls should be agreed before bulk orders are placed.
Where bedroom details repeat, reviewing a sample room or representative joinery assembly can help resolve issues before wider manufacture.
Later changes should be assessed for their cost, programme and operational implications before proceeding.
