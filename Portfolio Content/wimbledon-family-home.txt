A More Organised Approach to Family Living
This Wimbledon residence scheme connects shared living spaces with quieter private rooms through coordinated joinery, warm neutral colours and considered lighting.
The design addresses everyday family needs: somewhere to put household belongings, comfortable spaces for relaxing and entertaining, and a bedroom suite with clearly organised clothing storage.
In the living room, a media wall combines display shelving with closed cabinetry. The principal suite brings together fitted wardrobes, a dressing area and a coordinated bathroom, while the entrance establishes a calm, welcoming arrival.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on improving circulation, daylight and storage while accommodating both family routines and entertaining.
The principal objectives are to:
Create a clearer relationship between the ground-floor living spaces.
Make household storage part of the interior design.
Provide a comfortable reception room with integrated media equipment.
Organise the principal suite around sleeping, dressing and bathing.
Select finishes appropriate for frequent family use.
The design approach considers how the house functions throughout the day, from busy mornings to quieter evenings.
Design Challenges and Responses
Connecting Ground-Floor Spaces
A family home needs clear routes between the entrance, kitchen, dining area and garden-facing rooms.
Design response: Develop furniture layouts alongside circulation, checking door swings and routes through each space. Assess proposed alterations against the existing structure and services before finalising the design.
Keeping the Living Room Organised
Media equipment, books, toys and decorative objects place different demands on storage.
Design response: Combine open shelving for selected items with closed cupboards for everyday belongings. Coordinate equipment dimensions, ventilation, cable routes and maintenance access before cabinetry is manufactured.
Creating a Practical Dressing Room
A dressing room can look generous while providing unsuitable storage or restricted movement.
Design response: Establish the client’s hanging, drawer, shoe and accessory requirements first. Check wardrobe openings and circulation before introducing a dressing table, seating or central island.
Coordinating the Bedroom and Bathroom
The principal suite needs to feel connected without compromising privacy or practical access.
Design response: Plan the bed, wardrobes, dressing area and bathroom entrance together. Bathroom layouts should resolve usable clearances, storage, ventilation and maintenance requirements before finishes are selected.
Choosing Finishes for Daily Use
Kitchen, entrance and reception areas experience different levels of wear and moisture exposure.
Design response: Review flooring and surface samples against their intended use, cleaning requirements and supplier guidance. Where underfloor heating is proposed, confirm compatibility with the selected floor build-up and finish.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site discoveries, structural changes and client-requested revisions require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
Fitted Bedroom Storage
Full-height wardrobes create a consistent backdrop, with internal arrangements developed around the household’s clothing and storage requirements.
A Dedicated Dressing Area
Coordinated cabinetry, a dressing surface and suitable task lighting provide a distinct setting for daily preparation.
An Integrated Media Wall
Display shelving and closed storage bring several functions into one composition, reducing the need for separate furniture pieces.
A Considered Entrance Hall
A console, lighting and coordinated finishes establish the home’s design direction while preserving a clear arrival route.
Practical Kitchen Storage
A breakfast cupboard or pantry can keep frequently used items accessible. Its dimensions, ventilation and electrical requirements should be coordinated with the appliances it will accommodate.
