A Connected Workplace for Focus and Collaboration
This City of London office scheme brings together shared working areas, meeting rooms and informal seating within a coordinated interior.
Timber ceiling details give the open spaces a consistent identity, while glazed partitions define enclosed rooms without creating a visually disconnected layout. Upholstered lounge furniture introduces a softer setting for visitors and informal discussions.
The design approach considers how people move between activities throughout the day—from individual desk work to team meetings and client presentations.

The Workplace Brief
The supplied brief centres on adapting the workplace to different attendance patterns and activities.
The principal objectives are to:
Provide appropriate settings for focused and collaborative work.
Coordinate desk arrangements with power, data and storage.
Introduce enclosed rooms for meetings and presentations.
Create a welcoming reception and visitor lounge.
Maintain clear circulation between working areas.
Allow practical flexibility as team requirements change.
For a comparable project, attendance data, department relationships and meeting-room demand would inform the layout before desk numbers are fixed.
Design Challenges and Responses
Balancing Focused Work with Collaboration
Open-plan offices need to accommodate conversation without making concentration unnecessarily difficult.
Design response: Position collaborative areas with regard to quieter desks. Consider circulation, enclosed rooms and acoustic requirements together rather than relying on furniture screens alone.
Planning Daylight and Screen Positions
A bright office still needs careful workstation positioning to manage glare and reflections.
Design response: Coordinate desk orientation, monitor positions, blinds and artificial lighting. Check the arrangement against the actual windows and individual workstation requirements.
Making Flexibility Practical
Modular desks do not automatically make an office easy to rearrange.
Design response: Coordinate furniture with power, data, lighting and ventilation. Identify which areas can change without substantial alteration to services.
Providing Suitable Meeting Privacy
Glazed rooms can maintain visual connection, but confidentiality depends on the complete construction.
Design response: Define the privacy required for each activity. Coordinate partitions, doors, ceilings and service routes, obtaining specialist assessment where necessary.
A glazing rating alone should not be presented as a guarantee that conversations cannot be heard.
Coordinating Ceiling Elements and Services
Suspended timber features can affect lighting, ventilation and maintenance access.
Design response: Develop the ceiling layout alongside the building-services design. Confirm support arrangements, access requirements and any intended acoustic performance before manufacture.
Delivering Work within an Occupied Building
Commercial premises may have restrictions on deliveries, working hours and shared-area access.
Design response: Establish landlord requirements and site logistics early. Programme deliveries and disruptive activities around the agreed building arrangements.
These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
Timber Ceiling Detailing
Repeated slatted elements connect the open office and lounge settings. Their acoustic contribution should be confirmed against the specified construction.
Coordinated Workstations
Desk layouts, seating and cable management create an organised working area, developed around team requirements.
Glazed Meeting Rooms
Transparent divisions distinguish enclosed rooms while retaining visual continuity across the interior.
A Welcoming Reception Lounge
Soft seating and coordinated joinery give visitors a defined place to wait or meet informally.
Integrated Conference Technology
Furniture, screens, cameras and lighting should be coordinated to support presentations and hybrid meetings.

Managing Workplace Changes
Team numbers, attendance assumptions, meeting-room requirements and equipment schedules should be agreed before procurement begins.
Later changes should be reviewed for their cost, programme and technical implications before proceeding. This helps maintain a clear scope and gives the client an informed basis for decisions.
