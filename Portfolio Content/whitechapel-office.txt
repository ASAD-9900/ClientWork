A Workplace with Space to Focus, Meet and Collaborate
This Whitechapel office scheme brings together different working settings within a coordinated interior.
Open-plan workstations provide a shared working area, while enclosed meeting spaces support conversations and presentations. A reception setting welcomes visitors, and softer lounge seating offers an alternative to the desk.
Oak tones, olive upholstery, terracotta accents and dark framing introduce warmth and contrast. The design approach considers furniture, lighting, storage and technology together, so the workplace can support daily routines without unnecessary visual clutter.

The Workplace Brief
The supplied brief centres on accommodating focused work, team interaction and meetings within one workplace.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a practical workstation layout.
Provide appropriate settings for calls and meetings.
Create a welcoming reception.
Introduce comfortable breakout seating.
Coordinate power, data and meeting-room technology.
Allow flexibility for changing working patterns.
The starting point for a comparable project is understanding team size, attendance patterns and how different departments use the office.
Design Challenges and Responses
Balancing Collaboration with Concentration
Open-plan working needs to accommodate conversation without making focused tasks unnecessarily difficult.
Design response: Position collaborative settings away from quieter desk areas where possible. Consider enclosed rooms, furniture placement and acoustic requirements as part of the overall layout.
Providing Suitable Meeting Privacy
Glazed partitions preserve visual connection, but their appearance does not establish acoustic performance.
Design response: Define the privacy needed for each room. Coordinate the complete enclosure—including doors, ceilings, service routes and ventilation—with specialist advice where required.
Avoid promising confidential speech privacy without supporting assessment.
Integrating Power, Data and Equipment
Poorly coordinated services can lead to trailing cables, inaccessible connections and disruptive later alterations.
Design response: Establish desk positions, equipment requirements and connectivity before installation. Coordinate cable routes, access points and maintenance with the furniture and existing building services.
Selecting Appropriate Workstations
Desk layouts need to respond to the people and tasks they support.
Design response: Review desk dimensions, seating adjustability, monitor positions, lighting and circulation. Furniture selection should support individual workstation assessment rather than rely on an “ergonomic” label alone.
Introducing Breakout Space without Losing Useful Capacity
A lounge or café setting needs a clear purpose within the workplace.
Design response: Establish whether it supports breaks, informal meetings, individual work or several activities. Select furniture and locate power accordingly, while considering noise transfer to adjacent desks.
Working with an Industrial Interior
Exposed surfaces and visible services can influence sound, maintenance and the proposed fit-out.
Design response: Survey existing conditions before specifying treatment. Surface repairs, cleaning methods and acoustic interventions should be selected for the actual substrate and room.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
Coordinated Open-Plan Workstations
Desk arrangements and cable management create an organised working area, with dimensions and services developed around the team’s requirements.
Glazed Meeting Spaces
Transparent divisions maintain visual connection while defining separate settings for meetings. Required acoustic performance must be established independently.
A Welcoming Reception
A defined arrival point gives visitors a clear introduction to the workplace and supports practical access arrangements.
Comfortable Breakout Seating
Olive upholstery and warm finishes introduce a softer setting for breaks and informal conversation.
Integrated Meeting Technology
Screens, cameras, microphones and connectivity should be coordinated with room dimensions, furniture and lighting before installation.
