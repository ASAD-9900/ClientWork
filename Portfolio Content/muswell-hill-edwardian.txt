Edwardian Character with a Contemporary Design Direction
This Muswell Hill residence brings together the character of an Edwardian house and the practical requirements of a family home.
Decorative cornicing, fireplace details and a traditional hallway establish the architectural setting. The interior scheme introduces a complementary palette of sage green, soft neutrals, timber tones and brass details, connecting the reception rooms with the kitchen, bedrooms and bathrooms.
The kitchen and dining area provide a more contemporary focal point, while the formal rooms retain a quieter, more traditional identity. This balance allows different parts of the house to serve different purposes without feeling disconnected.

The Client Brief
The supplied brief centred on respecting the home’s Edwardian character while creating a lighter, more practical setting for family life and entertaining.
The principal requirements were to:
Retain the visual importance of original architectural features.
Create a sociable kitchen and dining space connected to the garden.
Coordinate colours and materials throughout the house.
Introduce bedroom storage suited to everyday use.
Develop comfortable bedrooms and practical bathrooms.
The design approach balances shared family spaces with quieter rooms, allowing the home to accommodate different activities throughout the day.
Design Challenges and Responses
Connecting Period Rooms with Contemporary Spaces
Older reception rooms and a contemporary rear addition can feel unrelated when their finishes are developed separately.
Design response: Use a shared palette and selected recurring details to connect the spaces. Sage tones, warm timber and brass accents provide continuity, while furniture and lighting respond to each room’s proportions.
Respecting Decorative Architectural Features
Cornicing, fireplaces and traditional joinery need room to remain visible within an updated interior.
Design response: Position furniture and proposed cabinetry around those features. Assess their condition before specifying repair, alteration or replacement, with specialist input where needed.
Balancing Open-Plan Living with Quieter Areas
A large kitchen and dining space supports entertaining, but a family home also benefits from places for conversation, reading and relaxation.
Design response: Give each room a clear purpose and consider how the spaces connect. Furniture arrangements, lighting and changes in texture can establish different settings without relying on additional partitions.
Planning Storage without Overcrowding Bedrooms
Large wardrobes and poorly positioned furniture can reduce usable space.
Design response: Plan bed position, circulation, and storage requirements together. Develop fitted furniture around the room’s dimensions, checking door openings, bedside access and internal storage before manufacture.
Coordinating Improvements to an Older Property
Existing floors, walls, windows and services may affect the proposed work.
Design response: Establish their condition through appropriate surveys before finalising the specification. Assess any insulation, glazing, or services upgrades for the specific building rather than selecting them based on appearance alone.
These describe the design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and construction solutions should be confirmed from project records.
Key Design Features
A Sage-Green Kitchen
Sage cabinetry provides a welcoming focal point, complemented by pale stone tones and warm metallic details. Detailed planning should coordinate preparation space, appliances, storage and seating.
Characterful Reception and Dining Rooms
Fireplace features and decorative detailing give the formal rooms their identity. Furniture, shelving and lighting should complement those proportions while supporting everyday use.
A Considered Entrance Hall
The hallway establishes the home’s design direction. Staircase details and patterned flooring can be balanced with practical lighting and discreet storage.
Restful Bedrooms
Soft neutral colours, upholstered elements and coordinated lighting create a calmer atmosphere, with storage planned around the household’s needs.
Coordinated Bathrooms
Stone textures and brass details connect the bathrooms to the wider palette. Practical specification must also address ventilation, cleaning, waterproofing and suitable finishes.
