An East London Dining Space with Warmth and Character
This Dalston restaurant scheme balances an industrial material palette with comfortable seating and warm lighting.
Terracotta-toned banquettes define the dining area, while dark tables provide contrast. An olive-green fluted counter gives the bar its own identity, complemented by brick textures, open shelving and metallic lighting details.
The design connects dining and drinks service within a consistent interior. Its character comes from the relationship between colour, texture and proportion, supported by practical planning for guests and staff.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating an approachable restaurant and evening bar with a distinctive local identity.
The principal objectives are to:
Combine dining and drinks service within one coordinated layout.
Provide comfortable seating without obstructing circulation.
Use perimeter space effectively through fitted banquettes.
Establish a recognisable bar through colour and detailing.
Select finishes appropriate for frequent commercial use.
Create an atmosphere suited to relaxed dining and evening visits.
Design Challenges and Responses
Balancing Seating Capacity with Comfort
Increasing table numbers can reduce usable space for guests and staff if clearances are considered too late.
Design response: Develop banquette positions, table dimensions and circulation together. Confirm capacity from the measured layout and relevant project assessments, rather than using an unsupported cover count.
Making Perimeter Seating Practical
Continuous banquettes can use wall space effectively, but their dimensions affect both comfort and access.
Design response: Coordinate seat height, depth, back support and table position before manufacture. Review sample upholstery and consider how seating will be cleaned and maintained.
Connecting Dining and Bar Service
The bar needs to support drinks preparation without creating congestion around nearby tables.
Design response: Position equipment, storage and service points around the operator’s menu and staffing model. Check routes for serving, clearing and replenishment during layout development.
Managing Sound around Hard Finishes
Brick, stone and timber surfaces can contribute to a lively sound environment.
Design response: Assess acoustic requirements alongside the ceiling, wall finishes and furnishings. Upholstery can contribute softness, but specialist acoustic performance should be specified and verified separately.
Coordinating Cooking and Building Services
The proposed menu influences equipment, ventilation, drainage and power requirements.
Design response: Establish the cooking methods and equipment schedule early. Coordinate specialist services against the premises and any neighbouring uses before finalising the fit-out.
Selecting Finishes for Spills and Wear
Tables, counters and upholstery experience frequent cleaning and repeated contact with food and drinks.
Design response: Review supplier information for suitability, maintenance and repair. Avoid promises that surfaces are stain-proof or that ventilation will eliminate odours without supporting evidence.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, installed systems and client-requested changes require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
Terracotta Banquette Seating
Warm upholstery establishes the dining room’s identity and creates a consistent backdrop for individual tables.
An Olive-Green Bar
Fluted detailing adds texture, while the contrasting colour makes the beverage counter a clear focal point.
Brick and Timber Textures
The combination introduces character without relying on excessive decoration.
Flexible Table Arrangements
Movable tables can accommodate different group sizes, subject to maintaining clear circulation and service access.
Warm, Layered Lighting
Table-focused lighting and wall fittings create smaller settings within the room, supported by practical lighting for staff duties and cleaning.
