A Professional Workplace with a Welcoming Client Experience
This London Bridge office scheme brings together the practical needs of a working team and the experience of visiting clients.
A defined reception provides a clear arrival point, leading towards meeting spaces and a dedicated lounge. Beyond the visitor-facing areas, open-plan workstations support daily team activity.
Warm oak tones, olive upholstery and soft neutral finishes create continuity throughout the interior. Dark-framed textured glazing distinguishes enclosed rooms while maintaining visual connection across the workplace.
The design approach considers circulation, furniture, storage and technology together, helping each setting serve a clear purpose.

The Workplace Brief
The supplied brief centres on balancing team working with client hospitality and confidential discussions.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a clear relationship between visitor-facing and staff areas.
Provide appropriate settings for meetings and focused work.
Coordinate open-plan desks with circulation and storage.
Integrate meeting-room technology and connectivity.
Create a comfortable client lounge.
Develop a consistent interior identity.
For a comparable project, team attendance, meeting frequency and confidentiality requirements would be established before finalising the layout.
Design Challenges and Responses
Separating Visitor Routes from Working Areas
Clients need to reach reception, meeting rooms and hospitality spaces without unnecessary movement through staff workstations.
Design response: Plan arrival and visitor circulation alongside the operational layout. Consider access arrangements, staff movement and the relationship between reception and meeting rooms.
Supporting Confidential Discussions
Visual screening and acoustic privacy are different requirements.
Design response: Define the level of privacy needed in each meeting room. Coordinate the complete enclosure, including doors, ceilings, partitions, service penetrations and ventilation, with specialist assessment where required.
Textured glass can obscure views, but should not be described as guaranteeing speech confidentiality.
Balancing Open-Plan Activity with Concentration
Conversation and movement can affect nearby desk users.
Design response: Position collaborative settings and circulation routes with regard to quieter working areas. Assess acoustic treatment as part of the room design rather than assuming that soft colours or timber finishes will control noise.
Integrating Conference Technology
Poor camera positions, lighting or audio arrangements can make meeting rooms difficult to use.
Design response: Coordinate screens, cameras, microphones, power and connectivity with the furniture layout. Check sightlines and lighting before installation, allowing access for maintenance.
Making Client Hospitality Practical
Refreshment points need to be convenient without interrupting meetings or creating clutter.
Design response: Plan storage, equipment and replenishment routes together. Select finishes around cleaning requirements and provide suitable service access.
Coordinating the Fit-Out with Existing Services
New enclosed rooms can affect lighting, ventilation and other building systems.
Design response: Survey the premises and coordinate specialist requirements before finalising partitions and ceilings. Confirm landlord interfaces and the condition of retained services.
These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
A Defined Reception
A timber-toned desk and coordinated finishes establish a welcoming arrival, with practical storage and equipment requirements integrated during detailed design.
Textured Glazed Divisions
Dark framing and reeded glazing distinguish meeting spaces while providing a degree of visual screening.
Coordinated Workstations
Desk arrangements, task seating and cable management should be developed around team requirements and individual workstation assessments.
Integrated Meeting Rooms
Furniture, lighting and technology work together to support presentations and hybrid meetings.
A Comfortable Client Lounge
Olive upholstery and warm joinery create an alternative setting for waiting and informal conversation.
