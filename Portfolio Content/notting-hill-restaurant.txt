Mediterranean Character with a Neighbourhood Feel
This Notting Hill restaurant scheme takes its direction from warm colours, curved architectural forms and contrasting textures.
Arched openings create a sequence of dining settings, giving the interior a sense of progression. Terracotta tones establish warmth, while olive seating, timber furniture and pale table surfaces introduce balance.
The hearth-cooking concept provides a potential focal point for the guest experience. Its successful integration depends on coordinating the dining layout with the cooking equipment, specialist services and operational requirements.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating a sociable Mediterranean dining environment with a series of smaller settings rather than one undifferentiated room.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a distinctive identity through arches, colour and texture.
Provide comfortable seating for different party sizes.
Connect guests visually with the cooking concept where appropriate.
Preserve clear circulation between dining areas.
Select finishes suitable for hospitality use.
Create a relaxed atmosphere through coordinated lighting.
Design Challenges and Responses
Creating Intimacy without Restricting Movement
Architectural divisions can give a restaurant character, but they must also support clear routes through the venue.
Design response: Coordinate archways, tables and service routes together. Check sightlines and access between the different dining settings before confirming capacity.
Integrating Hearth Cooking with the Dining Room
Wood-fired or charcoal cooking introduces specialist requirements beyond the decorative treatment of the kitchen.
Design response: Establish the equipment and fuel choices early, with appropriate specialist assessment of extraction, ventilation, fire precautions and the premises. The interior design should develop around the agreed technical solution.
Counter seating near cooking equipment must be assessed for its position, comfort and suitability.
Balancing Banquettes with Flexible Seating
Fixed booths can define intimate settings, while movable tables help accommodate different groups.
Design response: Develop seat dimensions, table positions and circulation together. Confirm practical group sizes from the measured layout rather than assuming every booth can seat four to six guests.
Selecting Textured Finishes for Commercial Use
Terracotta, plaster, timber and terrazzo create character but have different maintenance requirements.
Design response: Review the exact products, finishes and supplier guidance for each application. Assess flooring for expected conditions, counters for their intended use and table surfaces for cleaning and repair.
Keeping Conversation Comfortable
Hard floors and curved plaster surfaces can contribute to sound reflections.
Design response: Consider room acoustics alongside ceilings, walls and furnishings. Upholstery can contribute softness, but acoustic performance should be assessed rather than inferred from appearance.
Combining Atmosphere with Practical Lighting
Low-level lighting should create warmth while allowing guests and staff to use the space comfortably.
Design response: Combine decorative wall fittings with table, circulation and task lighting. Test the proposed settings during commissioning.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical solutions require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
Sculptural Arches
Curved openings frame views and distinguish individual dining settings while maintaining a consistent architectural language.
Terracotta and Olive Colours
Earthy wall tones and green upholstery establish the Mediterranean direction without relying on excessive decoration.
Timber Dining Furniture
Warm timber finishes connect the seating and tables, with proportions selected around comfort and the intended service model.
Contrasting Table Surfaces
Stone and terrazzo-style finishes introduce variation. Exact materials should be confirmed against the approved specification before publication.
A Hearth-Led Concept
Where technically feasible, visible cooking can become part of the restaurant experience, supported by coordinated equipment, services and seating arrangements.
