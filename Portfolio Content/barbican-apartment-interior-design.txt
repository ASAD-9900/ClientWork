Warmth and Practicality within an Architectural Setting
This Barbican apartment scheme explores how contemporary interiors can complement a strong architectural setting.
Walnut tones, pale stone surfaces and cognac-coloured seating establish a warm palette. Upholstered furniture and rugs introduce softness, while clearly arranged living, dining and kitchen areas support everyday use.
The design approach gives storage, furniture proportions and lighting equal consideration. The aim is a home that feels welcoming and organised, with a consistent relationship between its individual spaces.

The Design Objectives
The supplied brief centres on balancing architectural character with the practical requirements of city living.
The principal objectives are to:
Create clearly defined areas for cooking, dining and relaxing.
Introduce storage without overwhelming the living space.
Bring warmth through timber tones, upholstery and contrasting textures.
Develop lighting suited to both daytime activities and evening relaxation.
Coordinate proposed alterations with the building’s existing fabric and services.
Design Challenges and Responses
Organising Connected Living Spaces
An apartment’s shared spaces need to accommodate several activities while retaining comfortable routes between them.
Design response: Plan the kitchen, dining furniture and seating together. Check furniture dimensions, chair clearances and cupboard openings before finalising the layout.
This helps avoid an attractive arrangement becoming awkward in daily use.
Introducing Storage without Visual Clutter
Large storage units can dominate an apartment if their proportions and placement are considered too late.
Design response: Develop cabinetry around specific requirements, including media equipment, household belongings and kitchen storage. Combine closed cupboards with selected display areas to keep the overall composition balanced.
Bringing Warmth to a Strong Architectural Interior
A restrained palette needs sufficient variation to feel comfortable rather than flat.
Design response: Pair warm timber tones with lighter surfaces, cognac-coloured upholstery and tactile fabrics. Use rugs and upholstered seating to introduce softness without claiming an untested level of acoustic performance.
Coordinating Lighting with the Building Fabric
Lighting proposals must respond to the existing ceiling construction, service routes and permitted alterations.
Design response: Assess suitable surface-mounted fittings, lamps and integrated joinery lighting during detailed design. Confirm fixing methods and electrical requirements before installation.
Planning a Sensitive Refurbishment
Before work begins, the project team should establish the property’s applicable heritage, lease and building-management requirements.
Design response: Identify the relevant approvals, survey existing services and agree the proposed interventions before committing to manufacture or site work. Access arrangements, working hours and protection of communal areas should also inform the programme.
These are design considerations for this type of project, rather than verified accounts of difficulties encountered at this property.
