Contemporary Apartment Living with Warmth and Character
This Canary Wharf penthouse design brings a warmer material palette to a contemporary Docklands setting. Terracotta, rich timber tones and brushed brass provide contrast to the apartment’s extensive glazing, while stone textures introduce depth.
The scheme connects a main living area, dining space, reading corner and dedicated bar area. Each setting has a distinct purpose, with coordinated finishes helping the apartment feel cohesive.
The same design approach continues into the bathrooms, where earthy colours and metallic details create continuity with the living spaces.

The Design Objectives
The scheme addresses three priorities: giving open-plan living spaces a clear purpose, introducing warmth and connecting the rooms through a consistent palette.
Furniture placement defines areas for conversation, dining and quieter moments. Repeated timber tones, earthy colours and brass details create a relationship between these spaces without making every room identical.
For a homeowner planning a similar project, the starting point is understanding what needs to improve: furniture layouts, storage, lighting, finishes or the apartment as a whole.
Design Challenges and Responses
Defining Separate Areas Within an Open-Plan Layout
An open-plan apartment needs enough separation for different activities while retaining a comfortable flow.
The design approach uses furniture groupings and changes in texture to distinguish the main seating area, dining space and reading corner. Clear routes between these settings are an essential part of developing the layout.
Bringing Warmth to a Glazed Contemporary Interior
Large areas of glazing can create a strong visual contrast with the interior finishes.
Terracotta tones, timber finishes and upholstered seating introduce warmth and softness. Their combination gives the apartment character while allowing the windows and outlook to remain prominent.
Balancing Appearance with Everyday Practicality
A coordinated interior must also accommodate storage, maintenance and daily routines.
For a comparable scheme, detailed design would establish storage requirements, furniture dimensions, accessible power points and suitable finishes. Bathroom materials would be selected for their intended use, cleaning requirements and compatibility with the installation system.
Planning Work Within an Apartment Building
A high-rise refurbishment may involve building management approvals, restricted working hours and delivery arrangements.
These requirements should be checked before procurement and installation are programmed. Lift dimensions, access routes and protection of communal areas can affect how furniture, joinery and large surfaces are delivered.
These are considerations for planning a similar project; they are not a record of confirmed difficulties encountered at this property.

What Would a Similar Project Cost?
Provisional investment: £150,000–£350,000 excluding VAT.
This allowance is intended for a substantial apartment refurbishment involving interior design, internal finishes, selected kitchen or bathroom works, joinery, furniture, lighting and installation. The precise combination must be agreed before a tailored budget is prepared.
The main factors affecting cost are:
Floor area and the number of rooms included.
Whether kitchens and bathrooms are retained or replaced.
The extent of electrical, plumbing and lighting alterations.
The quantity and specification of fitted joinery.
Furniture, stone, sanitaryware and decorative lighting selections.
Building access and installation requirements.
The allowance excludes property acquisition, major structural alterations and exceptional building-related charges. VAT should be added where applicable, with a separate contingency allowance of 10–15%.
A furniture and decoration project covering fewer rooms would require a separate, smaller scope and budget.
