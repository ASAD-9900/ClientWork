The Salon Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on combining product browsing with a comfortable, practical appointment experience.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a recognisable reception and checkout point.
Integrate retail display without obstructing circulation.
Coordinate styling chairs, mirrors and storage.
Provide comfortable waiting and hair-washing settings.
Keep tools and cables organised.
Select finishes appropriate for salon use and cleaning.
The service offering, staffing and equipment schedule should inform the layout before styling capacity is confirmed.
Design Challenges and Responses
Connecting Retail and Salon Activity
Browsing customers and clients attending appointments may need to use the entrance differently.
Design response: Coordinate product displays, reception and waiting positions together. Keep the route to styling stations clear and provide enough space to pause without blocking circulation.
Creating Practical Styling Stations
A visually simple station still needs to accommodate tools, products and movement around the client.
Design response: Plan chair positions, mirror dimensions, work surfaces and storage together. Check working clearances with the selected chair in use before manufacturing joinery.
Coordinating Mirror and Task Lighting
Illuminated mirrors contribute to the interior’s character, but do not alone establish suitable lighting for hair services.
Design response: Review colour rendering, glare and shadows across the full lighting arrangement. Confirm product performance and assess the installed result.
Keeping Tools and Cables Accessible
Concealed equipment needs to remain convenient and safe to use.
Design response: Coordinate power points, cable routes and suitable tool holders with the equipment schedule. Provide access for maintenance and appropriate protection around heated tools.
Giving the Wash Area a Distinct Character
The wash setting needs to support reclining clients while remaining practical for staff.
Design response: Coordinate basin dimensions, staff access, lighting and services. Assess noise management separately rather than assuming a decorative partition provides acoustic isolation.
Selecting Flooring and Countertops
Salon products and frequent cleaning can affect surface finishes.
Design response: Confirm the exact flooring system, sealer and countertop specification against supplier guidance. Review exposure to the salon’s products rather than promising universal chemical or stain resistance.
These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
A Defined Reception
A coordinated desk provides a clear arrival point, with storage and equipment requirements integrated into its working side.
Arched Styling Mirrors
Repeated mirror forms create visual continuity across the styling area, supported by suitable task lighting.
Comfortable Client Seating
Upholstered lounge furniture offers a softer waiting setting, positioned around clear routes through the salon.
A Dedicated Wash Area
Reclining basins establish a distinct part of the appointment journey, with plumbing, hot-water demand and drainage assessed for the intended use.
Coordinated Product Display
Shelving can introduce retail products into the interior, with spacing, lighting and replenishment access developed around the merchandise.
A Considered Washroom
Open shelving, greenery and a stone-toned basin connect the supporting space with the wider palette, subject to practical cleaning and maintenance requirements.
