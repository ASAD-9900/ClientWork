A Family Home Connected to Its Garden
This Richmond house scheme centres on a welcoming ground floor with a stronger relationship between the interior and garden.
The design brings together sociable kitchen and dining spaces with a quieter living area. Muted green tones reference the surrounding planting, while walnut finishes, pale stone textures and warm metallic details introduce contrast.
Storage, furniture placement and lighting help define the different settings. The aim is a home that accommodates busy family routines while remaining comfortable for quieter evenings and entertaining.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief focuses on creating a family home with better-connected shared spaces, useful storage and a coordinated interior palette.
The principal objectives are to:
Strengthen the relationship between the kitchen, dining area and garden.
Provide distinct settings for family activities and relaxation.
Integrate storage into the architecture of the rooms.
Coordinate furniture, lighting and finishes throughout.
Balance visual character with practical maintenance.
The garden connection informs the layout and colour direction, while the living spaces provide a warmer, more sheltered setting within the house.
Design Challenges and Responses
Balancing Sociable Spaces with Quieter Living
A large connected ground floor needs to accommodate cooking, conversation and relaxation without every activity competing for the same space.
Design response: Establish clear zones through furniture placement, joinery and focal features. A fireplace can provide visual separation, but acoustic privacy requires a separate assessment and appropriate physical separation where needed.
Making Garden Access Work with Furniture Layouts
Large glazed openings can improve the outlook, but furniture must allow comfortable access between the house and terrace.
Design response: Plan dining positions, seating and circulation together. Check door operation, furniture clearances and routes to the garden before finalising the arrangement.
Providing Storage for Everyday Family Life
An attractive interior needs somewhere for the belongings that accumulate through daily use.
Design response: Combine closed cupboards with selected display shelving. Kitchen storage should be planned around food, appliances, preparation equipment and recycling, while living-room cabinetry can accommodate books and media equipment.
Selecting Practical Stone and Timber Finishes
Natural materials vary in their maintenance needs and suitability for different uses.
Design response: Confirm the intended application before choosing the material. Worktops, bathroom surfaces and flooring should be assessed for cleaning, moisture exposure, staining and the level of maintenance the household is comfortable undertaking.
Coordinating Proposed Alterations with the Existing House
Changes to openings, floors and services can affect the design, cost and programme.
Design response: Establish existing conditions through surveys and technical coordination before work begins. Structural alterations and heavy fixtures require project-specific assessment rather than assumptions based on appearance.
These describe the scheme’s design considerations. Actual site discoveries, structural solutions and client-requested revisions should be confirmed from project records.
Key Design Features
Walnut-Toned Joinery
Timber finishes bring warmth to the interior and connect storage, shelving and furniture across the shared rooms.
A Practical Kitchen and Dining Hub
The kitchen layout should coordinate preparation, cooking, washing-up and storage, with island dimensions established from the available floor space rather than a predetermined size.
Comfortable Living Spaces
Upholstered seating, textured furnishings and stone accents create a balanced setting for family use. Lighting can distinguish reading, conversation and evening relaxation.
A Coordinated Bathroom
Pale surfaces and warm metallic details continue the wider palette. Detailed planning should resolve storage, ventilation, waterproofing and access for maintenance.

Managing Design Changes
Layouts, kitchen arrangements, joinery drawings and principal finishes should be approved before manufacture or installation.
Any later change should be recorded with its cost and programme implications before proceeding. This helps the client understand the effect of each decision and maintain control of the agreed scope.
