A Neighbourhood Restaurant with Warmth and Character
This Greenwich restaurant scheme brings together a coordinated palette and a practical approach to all-day hospitality.
Olive upholstery and terracotta seating introduce colour, while timber tables and fluted joinery provide warmth and texture. Pale stone surfaces create contrast at the counter, giving the service area a clear identity within the dining room.
The design considers the experience from arrival to seating and service. A defined reception point, varied seating and coordinated lighting help establish a welcoming atmosphere for different types of visit.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating a neighbourhood venue that supports café service and a more relaxed evening dining experience.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a clear arrival and reception point.
Coordinate counter service with seated dining.
Offer comfortable seating for different group sizes.
Connect furniture, joinery and finishes through one palette.
Develop lighting suitable for different service periods.
Select materials appropriate for frequent commercial use.
Design Challenges and Responses
Balancing Counter Service with Seated Dining
Customers ordering drinks or collecting takeaway items can interrupt dining areas if their routes are not considered early.
Design response: Plan the entrance, ordering point, collection position and routes to tables together. The final arrangement should reflect whether the operator provides counter service, table service or a combination.
Creating Flexible Seating Arrangements
A restaurant needs to accommodate different parties without compromising circulation.
Design response: Coordinate banquettes with movable tables and chairs. Check table combinations, chair movement and service access before confirming seating capacity.
Making the Counter Practical to Operate
A distinctive counter must also support preparation, cleaning and replenishment.
Design response: Establish the menu and equipment schedule before detailing the joinery. Coordinate refrigeration, glasswashing, waste, power, plumbing and maintenance access.
Managing Sound around Hard Surfaces
Timber, stone and conversation can contribute to a lively dining environment.
Design response: Consider acoustic requirements alongside the ceiling, wall finishes and furniture. Upholstery and drapery may contribute to absorption, but neither establishes acoustic privacy or guarantees a particular result.
Coordinating Building Services with the Premises
Cooking methods and equipment choices affect ventilation, drainage and electrical requirements.
Design response: Survey existing services and obtain specialist input before finalising the fit-out. Any proposed extraction route or alteration to the building should be assessed for the actual property.
Creating an Evening Atmosphere
A setting that works for morning coffee may need adjustment for longer evening visits.
Design response: Combine ambient, table and task lighting with appropriate controls. Test visibility at tables, circulation routes and service points during commissioning.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
Olive and Terracotta Seating
Contrasting upholstery colours give the dining room character while maintaining a coordinated appearance.
Warm Timber Tables
Timber tones connect the loose furniture with the fitted joinery, creating continuity across the guest areas.
A Fluted Service Counter
Textured cabinetry and a pale countertop define the beverage service area. Exact timber and stone specifications should be confirmed before publication.
A Defined Reception Point
A clear arrival position helps guests understand where to wait, order or be seated.
Layered Lighting
Decorative and practical lighting work together to support daytime use, evening dining and cleaning.
