A Connected Interior for Contemporary Family Living
This Marylebone house scheme brings shared living spaces and private rooms together through coordinated joinery, soft furnishings and stone finishes.
Built-in shelving gives the bedroom a defined architectural backdrop, while the dressing room combines fitted storage with upholstered seating. In the living area, a fireplace provides a focal point, introducing a more traditional element within the wider scheme.
The kitchen island and freestanding bath offer distinct features in their respective rooms. Rather than repeating every finish throughout the house, the design approach creates continuity through complementary materials, proportions and lighting.

The Residential Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on adapting a townhouse interior for contemporary family use while retaining its architectural character.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a consistent design direction across the house.
Coordinate furniture and fitted joinery.
Improve bedroom and clothing storage.
Balance shared living spaces with quieter private rooms.
Develop practical kitchen and bathroom arrangements.
Consider lighting, maintenance and everyday use alongside appearance.
The condition and status of any historic features should be established before alterations are specified.
Design Challenges and Responses
Connecting Different Rooms
A whole-house scheme needs continuity without making every space identical.
Design response: Establish a shared palette of timber tones, upholstery colours and selected details. Adjust the furniture, lighting and finishes to suit each room’s purpose.
Balancing Storage with Comfortable Proportions
Large wardrobes and shelving can overwhelm a room if their dimensions are considered separately from the furniture.
Design response: Plan the bed, seating, storage and circulation together. Coordinate joinery with door openings, windows and existing architectural details.
Making the Dressing Room Practical
Display shelving and decorative seating should not reduce useful storage or access.
Design response: Establish hanging, drawer, shoe and accessory requirements first. Check wardrobe openings and circulation before introducing an ottoman or central furniture.
Working around Existing Architectural Features
Fireplaces, cornicing and other original details may influence furniture placement and proposed alterations.
Design response: Survey their condition and establish any applicable restrictions before finalising the design. Obtain specialist advice where repair or alteration requires it.
Coordinating Kitchen Materials with Daily Use
A statement island needs to support preparation, seating and cleaning.
Design response: Resolve dimensions, equipment and circulation before selecting the surface. Review the exact stone or composite material against the household’s maintenance expectations.
Planning the Bathroom around Selected Fittings
A freestanding bath affects circulation, cleaning access and service arrangements.
Design response: Confirm the fitting’s dimensions, weight and installation requirements. Coordinate floor capacity, plumbing, ventilation and access with the relevant specialists.
These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site discoveries, client revisions and completed restoration work require confirmation from project records.

Managing Design Changes
Room layouts, kitchen arrangements, wardrobe interiors and principal finishes should be approved before manufacture begins.
Later changes should be recorded with their cost and programme implications before proceeding. This gives the client a clear basis for decisions and helps maintain control of the agreed scope.
