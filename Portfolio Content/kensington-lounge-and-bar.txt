A Welcoming Lounge from Day to Evening
This Kensington hospitality scheme explores how one venue can accommodate quieter daytime visits and a more intimate evening atmosphere.
Architectural panelling and illuminated bar shelving establish the interior’s identity. Curved banquettes create smaller social settings, while terracotta-toned pendants and metallic table lamps introduce warmth.
The design approach connects the guest experience with practical operation: a clear arrival, comfortable seating, accessible service routes and a bar arranged around the operator’s requirements.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating a comfortable neighbourhood venue that can transition between daytime service and evening cocktails.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a distinctive bar and welcoming entrance.
Offer different settings for individual guests and groups.
Coordinate seating with clear staff and guest circulation.
Develop adjustable lighting for different service periods.
Integrate equipment and storage into the bar design.
Select finishes appropriate for frequent commercial use.
The interior should feel cohesive throughout the day while supporting the operator’s service model.
Design Challenges and Responses
Accommodating Different Service Periods
Daytime coffee service and evening cocktails place different demands on lighting, equipment and atmosphere.
Design response: Establish the menu and operating pattern early. Coordinate preparation areas and storage around both uses, with lighting settings that can change without compromising visibility.
Balancing Intimate Seating with Clear Circulation
Curved banquettes can create welcoming settings, but their placement must leave sufficient space for movement and service.
Design response: Develop seating, table sizes and circulation together. Check chair movement, access requirements and staff routes before confirming capacity.
Making the Bar Practical Behind the Counter
A visually striking bar must also accommodate preparation, cleaning and replenishment.
Design response: Coordinate refrigeration, glasswashing, ice, waste, power and plumbing with the equipment schedule. Confirm maintenance access before joinery manufacture.
Managing Sound in a Social Setting
Conversation, music and equipment noise can affect comfort within the lounge.
Design response: Consider acoustic requirements alongside the ceiling, wall finishes and furniture. Decorative fluting and upholstery should not be presented as proof of a particular acoustic result.
Specialist advice may be needed to assess both room acoustics and sound transfer to neighbouring spaces.
Integrating Services with Architectural Features
Lighting and ventilation proposals need to respond to the existing building.
Design response: Survey the premises and coordinate service routes before finalising ceilings and joinery. Any heritage constraints or approval requirements should be established for the actual property.
Selecting Materials for Hospitality Use
Seating, counters and flooring must accommodate repeated cleaning and everyday wear.
Design response: Review samples and supplier information against the intended application. Confirm upholstery suitability, surface maintenance and repair options before selection.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed systems require confirmation from project records.
