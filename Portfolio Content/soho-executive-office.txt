A Professional Workplace with a More Welcoming Character
This Soho office scheme combines practical working spaces with the warmth of a residential interior.
Timber joinery and illuminated display niches introduce character, while a defined reception provides a clear arrival point. Glazed divisions organise the interior without creating a visually enclosed corridor.
A kitchen island gives the breakout area its own focal point, offering a different setting from the desk. The design approach connects these spaces through coordinated finishes, lighting and furniture rather than treating each room separately.

The Workplace Brief
The supplied brief centres on creating an office that feels professional and discreet without relying on a conventional corporate appearance.
The principal objectives are to:
Establish a welcoming reception.
Coordinate shared working areas with enclosed rooms.
Integrate practical storage and equipment.
Introduce warm finishes and selected display details.
Provide a comfortable breakout and refreshment setting.
Establish appropriate privacy for the activities taking place.
For a comparable project, team numbers, visitor patterns and confidentiality requirements would be confirmed before finalising the layout.
Design Challenges and Responses
Balancing Openness with Privacy
Transparent divisions can maintain visual connection, but some activities need greater discretion.
Design response: Define the visual and acoustic privacy required for each room. Coordinate glazing, doors, ceilings and service routes against those requirements, with specialist assessment where needed.
A partition rating alone should not be presented as proof that conversations cannot be heard.
Creating Warmth without Losing Practicality
Residential-style finishes need to accommodate daily office use.
Design response: Select furniture, upholstery and surfaces around cleaning, durability and maintenance requirements. Use timber tones and lighting to introduce warmth while retaining suitable working conditions.
Integrating Equipment into Joinery
Technology can create clutter when power, cables and ventilation are considered too late.
Design response: Confirm equipment dimensions and connection requirements before detailing cabinetry. Provide access for maintenance and appropriate ventilation where equipment is enclosed.
Making the Breakout Area Useful
A kitchen island needs to support the intended workplace activities rather than serve only as a decorative feature.
Design response: Establish whether the area is for refreshments, informal meetings, meals or a combination. Coordinate seating, appliances, storage, power and cleaning arrangements accordingly.
Coordinating Lighting across Different Settings
Reception, desks, corridors and breakout areas require different lighting approaches.
Design response: Combine appropriate task lighting with ambient and accent lighting. Check glare, screen positions and the visibility of circulation routes during detailed design.
Working with Existing Building Services
New room divisions and kitchen facilities can affect ventilation, plumbing and electrical arrangements.
Design response: Survey the premises and coordinate specialist requirements before finalising the layout. Confirm landlord interfaces and the capacity of retained services.
These are design considerations relevant to the scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.
Key Design Features
A Defined Reception
A contemporary welcome desk and architectural lighting establish a clear arrival point for visitors.
Warm Timber Display Niches
Integrated shelving and accent lighting provide a place for selected objects, adding character without filling the working areas with decorative furniture.
Glazed Circulation Spaces
Slim-framed glazing defines rooms while retaining visual connection along the corridor.
Coordinated Open-Plan Working
Desk arrangements, storage and cable management should be developed around team requirements and individual workstation assessments.
A Statement Kitchen Island
A stone-finished island anchors the breakout setting. Exact material and appliance specifications should be confirmed before publication.
