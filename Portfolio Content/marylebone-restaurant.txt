Kitchen Theatre with Comfortable Dining
This Marylebone restaurant scheme explores the relationship between cooking, service and the guest experience.
The open-kitchen concept introduces activity and a focal point, while booth-style seating creates quieter settings within the dining room. Cognac and olive upholstery provide contrast, connected by warm timber tones and coordinated lighting.
The approach considers more than appearance. Table arrangements, staff routes, equipment requirements and storage need to work together so the restaurant can operate comfortably throughout service.

The Design Brief
The supplied brief centres on making the kitchen part of the dining experience while maintaining comfortable seating and practical service arrangements.
The principal objectives are to:
Connect guests visually with food preparation where appropriate.
Offer different seating experiences within one coordinated interior.
Keep routes between the kitchen and tables clear.
Integrate service storage and equipment.
Create lighting suited to dining, preparation and cleaning.
Select furniture and finishes appropriate for commercial use.
Design Challenges and Responses
Integrating an Open Kitchen
Visible cooking introduces sound, heat and activity into the dining environment.
Design response: Coordinate the kitchen interface and nearby seating with the kitchen designer and relevant building-services specialists. Cooking methods, equipment and ventilation requirements should inform the layout from the outset.
If open-flame cooking is proposed, its feasibility and specialist requirements must be established before the interior is finalised.
Keeping Service Routes Practical
Tables, counter seating and waiting positions can create congestion around the kitchen pass.
Design response: Plan serving, clearing and replenishment routes alongside the furniture arrangement. Position service stations where they support staff without obstructing guest circulation.
Balancing Intimate Seating with Flexibility
Fixed booths create distinct settings but need to accommodate comfortable access and practical cleaning.
Design response: Develop banquette dimensions, table heights and surrounding clearances together. Combine fitted seating with movable tables and chairs where the layout supports it.
Managing Sound in a Lively Dining Room
Conversation, equipment and tableware all contribute to the sound environment.
Design response: Consider acoustic requirements alongside ceilings, wall finishes and furnishings. Upholstery may contribute to absorption, but booth partitions should not be described as providing acoustic privacy without supporting assessment.
Selecting Materials for Daily Service
Dining surfaces and seating experience frequent spills, cleaning and wear.
Design response: Confirm the exact materials and finishes against supplier information. Review cleaning, staining, abrasion and repair requirements rather than promising scratch-proof or stain-proof performance.
Creating Atmosphere without Losing Visibility
Dining lighting needs to feel welcoming while supporting menus, movement and service.
Design response: Coordinate ambient, table, feature and task lighting, with suitable controls. Test the settings during commissioning.
These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site problems, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.

Managing Design Changes
The menu, cooking methods, equipment schedule and seating arrangement should be coordinated before manufacture begins.
Later changes should be reviewed for their cost, programme and technical implications before proceeding. This gives the operator a clear basis for decisions and helps maintain control of the agreed scope.
