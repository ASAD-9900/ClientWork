Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions.

01 Challenge

Creating a Clear Arrival

Patients need to understand where to check in without obstructing the entrance.

Design Response

Make the reception desk a visible focal point and coordinate waiting furniture around clear circulation. Consider signage, door openings and different mobility requirements.

02 Challenge

Balancing Comfort with Cleaning Requirements

Textured furniture and decorative joinery can create a welcoming appearance, but their suitability depends on location and use.

Design Response

Assess waiting-area upholstery separately from treatment-room finishes. Review cleaning methods, surface detailing and supplier information before selection.

03 Challenge

Coordinating Dental Equipment and Services

Treatment-room furniture must work with the dental chair, equipment and practitioner’s working positions.

Design Response

Develop the layout with the practice and specialist suppliers. Confirm equipment clearances, power, plumbing, suction and maintenance access before cabinetry is manufactured.

04 Challenge

Supporting Patient Privacy

Reception conversations and consultations may involve sensitive information.

Design Response

Consider seating distances, screen positions and opportunities for discreet conversations. Establish acoustic requirements for enclosed rooms and assess the complete construction where specialist input is needed.

05 Challenge

Decorative finishes or a partition rating alone should not be presented as guaranteeing confidentiality.

Locating Basins Appropriately A decorative basin and a clinical handwashing facility serve different purposes.

Design Response

Confirm the location and intended use before specifying the basin, tapware and surrounding surfaces. The terrazzo-style detail should not be described as a clinical handwash installation without supporting information.

06 Challenge

Coordinating Ventilation and Lighting

Treatment spaces require an approach that responds to the activities and equipment used.

Design Response

Obtain appropriate specialist input and coordinate services with the room layout. Decorative lighting should complement the necessary working illumination.

These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site discoveries, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Totteridge Dental Fit-Out Cost?

Provisional interior fit-out allowance: £150,000–£350,000 excluding VAT.

This range is intended for a defined scope involving reception, waiting areas, selected room divisions, cabinetry, finishes, furniture and agreed services alterations.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, room uses and specifications
  • Reception and waiting areas: Desk, seating, storage and finishes
  • Treatment-room cabinetry: Equipment interfaces, storage and access
  • Partitions and doors: Room separation, privacy and circulation
  • Finishes: Flooring, walls and surfaces appropriate to each activity
  • Lighting and services: Defined electrical, plumbing and ventilation work
  • Handover: Testing, snagging and agreed documentation

Dental chairs, imaging equipment, suction and compressor plant, decontamination equipment and specialist ventilation require separate quotations unless expressly included.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

6–10 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 6–10 months, with some stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and equipment requirements: 3–5 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 4–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and specialist coordination: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals and landlord review: Confirm for the premises and proposed activities
  • Procurement and manufacture: 8–16 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 10–16 weeks
  • Commissioning, snagging and handover: 2–3 weeks
