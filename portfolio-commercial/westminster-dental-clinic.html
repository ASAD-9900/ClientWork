Design Problem Solving

Design Challenges & Architectural Responses

Every project is shaped by physical site parameters, technical regulations, and tailored spatial solutions.

01 Challenge

Balancing Consultation Comfort with Practical Use

A consultation room needs to support conversation, record keeping and any examinations included in the appointment.

Design Response

Establish how the room will operate before selecting furniture. Coordinate seating, desk positions, equipment and storage around the provider’s procedures.

02 Challenge

Planning Dental Equipment and Working Clearances

The treatment chair, cabinetry and delivery equipment need to function as one arrangement.

Design Response

Develop the layout with the practice and specialist suppliers. Confirm equipment dimensions, practitioner access, service connections and maintenance requirements before manufacture.

03 Challenge

Coordinating Storage and Cleaning

Clinical supplies, instruments and everyday belongings require different storage arrangements.

Design Response

Plan cabinetry around the practice’s operating and cleaning procedures. Review surface detailing, access and compatibility with the products used.

04 Challenge

Brand names or a smooth appearance alone do not establish suitability.

Establishing Instrument-Processing Requirements Instrument handling and decontamination arrangements depend on the services provided.

Design Response

Confirm the required workflow with the practice and relevant specialist advisers before planning supporting rooms. Do not describe separate clean and dirty routes unless the approved layout establishes them.

05 Challenge

Supporting Patient Privacy

Consultations and treatment may involve sensitive conversations.

Design Response

Define visual and acoustic privacy requirements for each room. Assess partitions, doors, ceilings and service routes together where specialist input is needed.

06 Challenge

Decorative timber panels should not be presented as proof of sound isolation.

Distinguishing Clinical and Decorative Basins A patient washroom vanity serves a different purpose from a clinical handwashing point.

Design Response

Specify each installation for its intended use, with appropriate consideration of cleaning, access and maintenance.

These are design considerations relevant to the supplied scheme. Actual site discoveries, client revisions and installed technical systems require confirmation from project records.

01 Investment Guide

What Would a Similar Westminster Clinic Fit-Out Cost?

Provisional interior fit-out allowance: £180,000–£400,000 excluding VAT.

This range is intended for a defined scope involving consultation and treatment-room interiors, selected partitions, cabinetry, finishes, furniture and agreed services alterations.

  • Design and technical coordination: Surveys, layouts, room uses and specifications
  • Consultation spaces: Furniture, storage, finishes and equipment interfaces
  • Treatment-room cabinetry: Fitted units, worktops and access requirements
  • Partitions and doors: Room separation, privacy and circulation
  • Supporting spaces: Defined washroom, storage and staff facilities
  • Lighting and services: Agreed electrical, plumbing and ventilation works
  • Handover: Testing, snagging and agreed documentation

Dental chairs, imaging equipment, radiation-protection works, suction and compressor plant, decontamination equipment and specialist ventilation require separate quotations unless expressly included.

02 Delivery Schedule

How Long Would a Similar Project Take?

6–12 months

For initial planning, allow approximately 6–12 months, with some stages overlapping.

  • Brief, surveys and equipment requirements: 3–5 weeks
  • Layout planning and concept design: 4–6 weeks
  • Detailed design and specialist coordination: 6–10 weeks
  • Approvals and landlord review: Confirm for the premises and proposed activities
  • Procurement and manufacture: 8–16 weeks or longer for selected items
  • Site fit-out and installation: 10–20 weeks
  • Commissioning, snagging and handover: 2–3 weeks
  • Specialist equipment, services alterations and approval requirements may extend the programme.: The opening date should also allow for the provider’s operational preparations. Remaining open during construction requires a separately assessed delivery plan.
